This note is verified. The series is NP EXPLAINS. The carousel was posted at 9:14 am IST on 31 October 2026. The stamp is VERIFIED. The cover photograph is courtesy of ISRO.

GSLV-F18 lifted off from the Second Launch Pad at Sriharikota. On the ISRO mission page, this is the 20th flight of the GSLV, and NVS-03 was placed in its intended geosynchronous transfer orbit. The same page says the satellite carries an indigenous atomic clock and procured atomic clocks, and that the L1 signal is added to L5 and S. L1 is the new signal. Those points are the ones the mission-page text supports.

The clock time, the minutes after lift-off, the orbit in kilometres, and the weight are not on that ISRO page text. They come from the news reports: The Times of India, The Indian Express citing PTI, and Space.com. Lift-off was at 11:51 pm IST on 30 October 2026. About 18 minutes later, NVS-03 was placed in a geosynchronous transfer orbit described as about 170 by 37,000 km. The satellite's weight is about 2,250 kg.

The caption says NavIC, India's own navigation system, needs at least four working satellites for positioning. The reports say NVS-04 and NVS-05 are in advanced stages. Those lines are also from the news reports, not from the ISRO mission-page text checked here.

ISRO chairman V Narayanan says the satellite is healthy and that its solar panel has been deployed. That is his remark, as carried in the news reports. It is not quoted from the ISRO mission-page text.

Read together, the split is this. From ISRO: the 20th GSLV flight, the Second Launch Pad at Sriharikota, placement in the intended geosynchronous transfer orbit, an indigenous atomic clock plus procured atomic clocks, and L1 added to L5 and S. From the news reports: 11:51 pm IST on 30 October, about 18 minutes to placement, an orbit of about 170 by 37,000 km, a weight of about 2,250 kg, at least four working satellites for NavIC positioning, NVS-04 and NVS-05 in advanced stages, and the chairman's remark that the satellite is healthy and the solar panel is deployed.

The sources named on the carousel are ISRO, The Times of India, The Indian Express (PTI) and Space.com.
