This note is verified. The reel's hashtag is NPExplains. GOV.UK's press release of 15 October 2026, and Via Satellite of the same day, were read in full at about 3:03 pm IST on 17 October. SpaceNews of 15 October returned only a lead paragraph. The rest of that article was not retrieved, so only that lead is used. The reel was posted at about 10:19 am IST on 18 October.

The UK Space Agency has chosen ClearSpace UK for its Active Debris Removal mission, which the release calls UK ADR. GOV.UK says the government backing is more than £60 million, and that ClearSpace UK is the industry delivery partner. Via Satellite says ClearSpace is the prime contractor, on a contract of £61 million, which it also gives as 80.7 million dollars. The SpaceNews lead that was retrieved says the agency picked ClearSpace's British unit as prime contractor for a 2029 mission. The two money figures stay side by side. This note does not collapse "more than £60 million" into £61 million.

What the spacecraft is to do, in the GOV.UK release, is catch two defunct UK satellites with robotic claws and release them lower in the atmosphere, where they burn up. The target in that release is to remove both by 2032. GOV.UK says this would be the first time a spacecraft disposes of more than one satellite in this way. That sentence is not a claim that no debris has ever been removed before. Via Satellite says the two satellites are UK-registered and defunct, in low Earth orbit, and that operations are targeted for 2029. The SpaceNews lead says the mission is to de-orbit two defunct small satellites.

The launch plan is 2029, from SaxaVord Spaceport in Scotland. That is the GOV.UK line. Via Satellite's line is that operations are targeted for 2029. Neither source gives a day or a month, and this note does not add one. The contract was announced on 15 October, and GOV.UK says a public competition to name the spacecraft is open.

GOV.UK says debris travelling at more than 17,000 mph threatens GPS, weather forecasts and communications. The caption adds a line for readers in India: India's navigation and weather satellites also work in Earth orbit, so a safer orbit matters beyond this one mission. The same caption says India is not part of the mission. That India sentence is general context. It is not a role for India in the contract.

The claw in the reel is not the final UK craft. It is a concept animation of an earlier ClearSpace design, from ClearSpace SA, distributed through ESA. The package labels it that way, and the caption says the same. The shots of Earth are representational footage by Adis Resic, via Pexels. The music is Metriko, "Somber Constellations Ambient", from Pixabay. The package says that track does not need a credit.

What was announced on 15 October is a UK mission, a partner, a sum of money that the two outlets phrase differently, a 2029 target, and a 2032 removal target. What the picture is not is the finished spacecraft.
