This note is NP NOW. The stamp is verified. It was last checked at 11:35 am IST on 11 October 2026 and posted at 11:33 am IST.

On 10 October 2026 a court at Karkardooma convicted Tahir Hussain of abetment of rioting in the Dayalpur case from February 2020. The sections named for that conviction are IPC 147 read with 109. Twelve co-accused were acquitted. The Hindu, PTI, Hindustan Times, LiveLaw and Bar & Bench report the verdict. The Hindu's report is timed at 7:06 pm IST on 10 October. PTI's copy is the one carried by Business Standard. The check also names NDTV and ThePrint for that PTI copy. Those two are not given as separate links here.

The wording is not identical, and this note keeps the difference. The Hindu says he was found "guilty of rioting and abetment." PTI and LiveLaw say "abetment to rioting," under IPC 147 read with 109. The screen says "abetting a riot." The conviction this note states is abetment of rioting. That is the PTI and LiveLaw line, and it is the caption. The Hindu's phrase sits beside it. This note does not turn the two phrases into two separate convictions.

He was acquitted of the other charges. The record used here names arson, under section 436, property damage, under section 427, and conspiracy, under section 120B. The screen's line is that he was not guilty of arson, of property damage and of conspiracy. The twelve co-accused were acquitted. Their names are not in this note.

No sentence is stated. None of the reports used here gives one. Arguments on sentence are listed for 16 November. That date is The Hindu, PTI and LiveLaw. Hindustan Times' body text says the matter is to be "heard separately on October 16." The notes on this story treat the October date as a typo and use 16 November. This note does the same. It does not state a punishment.

The judge's name is spelled two ways. The Hindu and PTI use Praveen Singh, Additional Sessions Judge. Hindustan Times and LiveLaw use Parveen Singh. The caption uses Praveen. Both spellings are on the record. This note does not pick a correction.

One finding about how the abetment was described is Hindustan Times alone, among the extracts checked. Hindustan Times, quoting a 64-page judgment, reports that the court found he allowed rioters to use the terrace of his house. The same reading says no witness placed him in the mob, and that the other charges failed for that reason. LiveLaw and Bar & Bench, in the extracts used for this note, do not carry the terrace line. The chip on the reel names Hindustan Times for the judgment. This note does the same.

The case on these pages is the Dayalpur case from February 2020. The verdict date is 10 October 2026. The conviction is abetment of rioting. The acquittal is of the other charges, and of the twelve co-accused. The next date is 16 November, for arguments on sentence. Nothing in this note is a sentence.
