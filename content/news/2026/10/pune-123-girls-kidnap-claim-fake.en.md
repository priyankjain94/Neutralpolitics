A reel and an audio clip claimed that 123 girls were kidnapped in Pune in 22 hours. That claim is unverified. It is alleged in the clip, and it is not a police finding. Free Press Journal, Lokmat and Punekar News reported it as a viral claim. This is Viral or Verified number 1, under the NP VERIFY label. The stamp on that claim is misleading.

As police summarised the clip, Free Press Journal reports, it said a woman was told at a police station that 123 similar cases had come in. That sentence is a summary of what the clip alleged. It is not a police tally. It is not a count this note has verified.

On 2 October 2026, Deputy Commissioner of Police (Crime) Gauhar Hasan spoke on the claim. Punekar News reports his statement. No concrete information, record or evidence had come to police establishing that 123 girls were abducted in Pune city in the past 22 hours. He called the claim "factually incorrect and baseless". In a video message, Free Press Journal quotes him: "No truth was found in the claim. It is completely fake." Those are his words, as those two outlets reported them. Punekar News is dated 2 October. Free Press Journal is dated 3 October.

Police described the checks. They contacted the person who recorded the audio. They spoke to society office-bearers, neighbours and residents. They checked with police stations and the Crime Branch. They found no record, no complaint and no evidence. Punekar News, DCN, Free Press Journal and Lokmat reported those checks. DCN and Free Press Journal are dated 3 October. Lokmat, in Marathi, is dated 2 October. This note does not add a place name, a society name or a station name. The reports used here do not supply those as findings beyond the checks just listed.

What remains open is the clip itself. Police say an inquiry into its origin and spread continues. Legal action can follow if new evidence emerges. No FIR against anyone has been reported. No action against anyone has been reported. That is how Free Press Journal, Punekar News, Lokmat and DCN leave it. These reports also do not give a count of real missing-person cases in the city. This note does not invent one. A police finding that this particular claim is false is not a census of other cases.

One piece of context sits beside the claim, and it is not the police statement. The Supreme Court has ordered FIRs for every missing-person report. The Hindu's report of that order is dated 22 May 2026. An August account, in The Economic Times, says the direction covers all ages. Such an FIR is not proof of abduction. Free Press Journal suggests the figure of 123 may have been confused with this. Police did not say so. This note keeps that suggestion as Free Press Journal's, not as a finding by Pune Police.

Neutral Politics checked these outlets on 4 October 2026 (IST). This note was posted at 11:15 am IST. As of about that time, no later Pune Police FIR, no finding on who made the clip, and no change in the 2 October statement had been reported since the morning of 4 October. The claim was still being called fake and baseless.
