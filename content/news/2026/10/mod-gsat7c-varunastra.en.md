This note is NP EXPLAINS. The stamp is verified. The Press Information Bureau release from the Ministry of Defence is timed 3:22 pm IST on 15 October 2026. The caption's check is 11:00 am IST on 16 October, and the reel went up at 3:42 pm IST. The Hindu, The Times of India and DD News also carry the contracts.

The Ministry signed two contracts that together come to ₹4,106 crore. The first is ₹2,563 crore with New Space India Limited, the commercial arm of ISRO, in Bengaluru. It is for GSAT-7C, a dedicated military communication satellite for the Indian Air Force. In the caption's plain words, that is a secure relay in space, so that jets, radars, air bases and command can speak to one another over distance. This note stays with that description.

The second contract is ₹1,543 crore with Bharat Dynamics Limited, in Hyderabad, for Varunastra heavyweight torpedoes for the Navy. The Press Information Bureau describes Varunastra as a ship-launched anti-submarine torpedo. The Naval Science and Technological Laboratory, a DRDO laboratory, designed it. The two sums are the sums in the release. This note does not add a count of torpedoes. That number has not been officially stated.

The signing was on 15 October 2026 in New Delhi, at the Ministry of Defence. Director General (Acquisition) A. Anbarasu was present.

A launch date for GSAT-7C has not been officially stated. The reel's note records that gap, and this article does not fill it.

The Times of India supplies one piece of context, and it stays attributed. It says the Air Force now relies on GSAT-7A, launched in 2018, which is in about the last stretch of an eight-year life, and that about 30 percent of that satellite's capacity is shared with the Army. That is The Times of India's account of an ageing satellite.

The Ministry presents the contracts as a step toward Aatmanirbhar Bharat. The caption's line is that public money is going to systems made by Indian companies and laboratories. Both of those are the Ministry's and the caption's framing of why the sums matter. They are not a claim about a particular conflict.

The reel uses file and stock pictures, labelled as such. Two stills are Press Information Bureau photographs from the 15 October release, under GODL-India, credited "Courtesy: PIB". A third is a file photograph from the Varunastra handover on 29 June 2016, Ministry of Defence, on Wikimedia Commons, GODL-India. The moving pictures are representational footage of the Indian Navy, Wikimedia Commons, GODL-India, and they are not the contracts or these torpedoes. A further clip is file footage of the GSAT-11 launch in December 2018 by Arianespace, CC BY 3.0. It is not GSAT-7C. The remaining cards are graphics made by Neutral Politics. The credit for those is "NP graphic".

What is verified is the day, the place, the two prices, the two contractors, and the stated roles of the satellite and the torpedo. What is context is the age of GSAT-7A, as The Times of India gives it. What has not been officially stated is a launch date for GSAT-7C, or a number of torpedoes.
