This note is NP NOW. The stamp is verified for Shipping Minister Sarbananda Sonowal's statement and for UKMTO incident 160. It was last checked at 8:52 am IST on 10 October 2026 and posted at 9:40 am IST. The attacker is not identified. This note does not name one.

Sonowal said on X that the tanker MT GEM NO. 2, which is Panama-flagged, was hit in a drone attack at Sharjah Anchorage on 9 October. He said the ship had 22 crew, all of them Indian, and that all 22 are safe. He said the government is monitoring the case and is in touch with the ship's management. The Tribune, citing ANI, and Times Now carry that account. Times Now's report is timed at 8:20 am IST on 10 October. The points used here are the ones those reports attribute to him: the ship's name, the flag, the place, the date, the drone, the crew of 22, their safety, and the government's contact with the ship's management.

UKMTO incident 160, dated 9 October 2026, is the incident record. It says a vessel about 13 nautical miles west of Al Jazeera, in the UAE, was hit by an unknown projectile. It says a fire started and was later put out. It says the crew, the damage and the environmental impact are unknown, and that the authorities are investigating. Those three unknown lines are UKMTO's. This note does not add a count of injuries, and it does not add a finding about the environment. Sonowal's account and the UKMTO note sit side by side. He says the 22 Indian crew are safe. UKMTO says the crew's status, on its own record, is unknown.

Moneycontrol, citing Reuters, reports that Reuters sources identify the vessel in UKMTO incident 160 as GEM NO. 2. Sonowal's statement names the same ship. The identification used here is those two accounts. The compiled account also describes the ship as a crude oil tanker.

The compiled facts put the strike at about 1000 GMT on 9 October, about 3:30 pm IST, and say the tanker was anchored and fully laden. The source list does not assign that clock, or the laden line, to a single outlet. The date, 9 October, is the date in Sonowal's account and in the UKMTO incident. Sharjah Anchorage is the place Sonowal names. The 13 nautical miles west of Al Jazeera is UKMTO's place line.

Sonowal's word for the weapon is a drone. UKMTO's word is an unknown projectile. Both are reported as said. No claim of responsibility is recorded. The attacker is unknown. UKMTO says the authorities are investigating, and the note's own line is the same question, still open.

Two tracks, then, and neither one fills the other's gap. The minister's track is the crew: 22 Indians, all safe, government in touch with the ship's management. The UKMTO track is the incident: an unknown projectile, a fire that was put out, and crew, damage and environmental impact left unknown. Reuters sources, through Moneycontrol, tie incident 160 to GEM NO. 2, which is the name Sonowal uses.
