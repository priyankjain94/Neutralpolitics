This note is NP NOW. The stamp is verified. It was last checked at 12:50 pm IST on 11 October 2026 and posted at 12:50 pm IST.

The Supreme Court sat specially on Sunday, 11 October 2026. The bench was Justices Joymalya Bagchi and V. Mohana. Hindustan Times' live report confirms that bench. What this note uses for their words is "said." The written order was still awaited at the check.

NDTV reports that the bench said all the barricades should be removed unless absolutely warranted. It reports that the bench said free movement should be normalised, subject to regulation where public order requires it. It reports that the bench said the right to protest is there, within the Police Act. Those three lines are the screen. They are what NDTV attributes to the bench as things it said.

Bar & Bench's live account is the other wording in hand. It records dictated directions. Those include that free movement is to be permitted, and that barricades imposed shall be reviewed, and reviewed forthwith. It records that the matters are tagged with the main matters on Tuesday, and that a compliance report is due by Tuesday, 13 October. The notes on this story treat that gap as a gap: NDTV's "should be removed unless absolutely warranted," and Bar & Bench's "reviewed forthwith." The screen follows the first. This note does not treat those spoken lines as the written order, because that order was not yet uploaded.

Two pleas were before the sitting, and this note keeps them as pleas. A contempt plea by Shailendra Mani Tripathi alleges that the Metro and the railways were shut despite the 9 October order. A habeas plea concerns people who were detained. The Solicitor General disputed the shutdown allegation and told the court the Metro ran normally. This note does not adopt the plea's allegation as what happened. It also does not name anyone who was detained.

What the barricades were doing on Sunday morning is a separate report. Hindustan Times, that morning, said barricades remained at Jantar Mantar and at the borders. This note does not say they have come down. "Should go, unless absolutely warranted" is what the bench is reported to have said. It is not a report that they have gone.

The next date on Bar & Bench is Tuesday, 13 October, for the tagged matters and the compliance report. Until the written order is up, the two phrasings stand side by side: NDTV's "said ... unless absolutely warranted," and Bar & Bench's "reviewed forthwith."
