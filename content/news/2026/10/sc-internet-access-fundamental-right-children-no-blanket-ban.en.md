This note is NP EXPLAINS. The stamp is verified. The judgment, 2026 INSC 1149, dated 15 October 2026, was read for paragraphs 292, 297, 299 to 302, 316 and 320. The Hindu, Hindustan Times and The Indian Express were read at about 2:59 pm IST on 17 October 2026. The Indian Express page was updated at 2:57 pm IST. The carousel was posted at 3:49 pm IST. The check ran until about 3:02 pm IST.

The order is in M.A. Nos. 2553-2554 of 2026 in criminal appeals 2161-2162 of 2024, Just Rights for Children Alliance v. S. Harish. It is signed by Justices J.B. Pardiwala and K.V. Chandran and dated 15 October 2026. Hindustan Times and the order signature style the second judge as K.V. Chandran. The Hindu styles him Vinod K. Chandran. The package treats these spellings as the same judge, Justice K. Vinod Chandran. Hindustan Times reports that the 15 October order was released on 17 October.

Paragraph 292, in the stamp basis taken from the judgment, holds that access to and use of the internet is a fundamental right under Article 21 read with Article 19(1)(a). The caption records the court's sentence: "Our children, irrespective of their age enjoy the same rights." The stamp basis says children are "entitled to both," irrespective of their age. Article 21 is the right to life and personal liberty. Article 19(1)(a) is freedom of speech and expression. The caption describes the right as belonging to every person, with children included.

Paragraph 297 calls a blanket ban on children's internet use "unproven measures of proven disproportionality." The caption says such a ban can push children onto less-regulated platforms. The court is not against age-based safeguards. Paragraphs 299 to 301 say the court is not against age-based restrictions. They name parental consent, guidance and oversight, and a "child mode": an account private by default, limits on contact from unknown adults, and moderation of ads and content. The modalities are left to the Union. The caption says the Centre will decide the details.

Paragraph 302 says the Union may restrict inherently harmful content, for example adult websites. The Indian Express confirms that the government can restrict harmful content, including adult sites. The Hindu confirms the holding, the quoted lines, and that the court is not against age-based restrictions, parental consent and a child mode. Hindustan Times confirms the Article 21 read with 19(1)(a) holding and the child sexual abuse material context. The stamp basis shortens that material to CSEAM.

Paragraph 316 sets out a framework, as the stamp basis records it. A national Hash and URL List Database, with three trained analysts to verify entries. Platforms cross-check the database and remove matches. Removal is to be reported within two hours when the origin is unknown, and weekly when an item matches the database. A report option for CSEAM is to sit within two steps. An acknowledgement is to go out within 24 hours with a reference number. The Union is to consider exemplary fines. The awareness section of the order names helplines 1098 and 1930. The caption names them as Childline 1098 and the Cyber Crime Helpline 1930. Paragraph 320 lists the matter on 10 December 2026.

What the order did not do is in the caption, and this note repeats that list as the caption's limit on the ruling. No law was struck down. Age limits were not banned. Social media was not made free for every age.

The Indian Express names the applicants as Just Rights for Children Alliance and confirms the bench, the 15 October date, and a national database of identifiers and URLs. The PDF used here is the copy carried by Verdictum. Child-user counts are not in the sources used for this note.
