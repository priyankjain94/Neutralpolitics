This note is NP EXPLAINS. The stamp is verified. It was checked at 3:10 pm IST on 16 October 2026, rechecked at about 5:45 pm IST against the PIB, The Hindu, News on AIR and the other reports used here, and posted at 5:48 pm IST.

Vice-President C.P. Radhakrishnan commissioned INS Samarthak into the Indian Navy on 16 October 2026 at Naval Base Karwar, in Karnataka. The Chief of the Naval Staff was present. The Hindu, News on AIR, the Press Information Bureau and The New Indian Express report the commissioning. The Navy's own post on X is among the sources.

The ship is described as the Navy's first indigenous multi-purpose vessel. Larsen & Toubro built it under Make in India, which the caption glosses as a private Indian shipyard. Indigenous content is stated as more than 70 percent. Some reports say 75 percent. This note prints both and does not choose one.

The caption lists what the ship is for. It is a trials platform for Indian sensors and weapons. It is for maritime surveillance and patrol. It is for humanitarian assistance and disaster relief, the work the caption shortens to HADR. It is a mothership for drones and unmanned systems. Those are the roles named. This note does not add missions beyond that list.

How many sister ships follow is not one figure. The Navy's account, which the package also ties to a Navy or PIB post on X, is that this is the first of four multi-purpose vessels. Some reports, including the text of a PIB release of 13 October, say the first of two. The reel puts the conflict on screen rather than erasing either line. This article does the same. Neither number is treated as the only one.

News on AIR's account is that the Vice-President commissioned the indigenously built ship in Karnataka. The Hindu calls INS Samarthak a multi-purpose vessel commissioned into the Navy. The New Indian Express reports the Vice-President and the ship at Karwar. Those lines are the verified core. The percentage, and four ships or two, stay open beside them.

The caption's reason for the attention is that warships built in the country mean Indian jobs and less reliance on defence imports. That is the caption's line.

The reel's footage is the Indian Navy's, from @IndiannavyMedia, with the Navy's audio kept. A still used near the end is a Press Information Bureau photograph from 13 October 2026, GODL-India, credited "Courtesy: PIB".

What is verified is the commissioning, the place, the day, who commissioned the ship, and who built it. What the reports do not settle is the exact indigenous share, and whether the class is four ships or two.
