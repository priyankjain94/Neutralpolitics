This note is NP VERIFY. The stamp is verified for one finding: this was not a hijack. The cause is developing. It was rechecked at 4:55 pm IST on 15 October 2026, and the post went up at 5:00 pm IST.

Developing: no official cause has been announced.

The flight was Air India AI2030, from Frankfurt to Delhi, on 14 October 2026. The aircraft was a Boeing 787-9. On board were 306 people: 293 passengers and 13 crew. It was diverted to Jaipur after a 7500 transponder code showed on radar. The Indian Express, The Times of India, India Today, BusinessLine, with PTI, and Hindustan Times report the diversion and the alert.

A squawk code is the four-digit code a pilot sets in the transponder, the radio box on the aircraft. Code 7500 means "unlawful interference", which is a hijack. Controllers treat 7500 as a hijack and act on that reading. That is what the code is for. It is not, by itself, a finding that a hijack is under way.

What happened next is the verified sequence. The plane landed safely in Jaipur at 9:35 am. The 306 people were checked and cleared. The flight then continued to Delhi. The pilots told controllers that operations were normal, and they denied setting the code. The Times of India, BusinessLine and The Indian Express report that account from the pilots.

The alert was not a real hijack. That is the verified stamp. The people on board were cleared, and the aircraft went on to its destination.

The cause is a separate question, and it is not closed. DGCA and security agencies are probing. Two lines are in the reports, and this note does not choose between them.

The Times of India reports that Mumbai ATC's new radar system is being looked at. Security sources speaking to PTI call a glitch possible. That is a line about what the probe is examining. It is not an announced finding that the radar system produced the code.

The Indian Express quotes an official saying a preliminary report suggests the pilot may have set the code by mistake. That is the official's account of what the preliminary report suggests, as The Indian Express carries it. It sits next to the pilots' denial that they set the code. This note does not treat the suggestion as a finding, and it does not treat the denial as a finding either. Both are reported.

The recheck at 4:55 pm IST on 15 October left the cause where it was. No official cause has been announced. The stamp therefore stays verified on the alert, and developing on the cause.

The cover is a graphic made by Neutral Politics, with an NP-made map from Natural Earth. The credit is "NP graphic". No photograph is used.

What is verified is the flight, the aircraft, the 306 people, the 7500 code on radar, the safe landing at 9:35 am, the checks and the clearance, the onward flight to Delhi, the pilots' account to controllers, and the finding that this was not a hijack. What is developing is why the code appeared. A later announcement from DGCA or the security agencies would be the official cause. Until that announcement, the radar line and the preliminary-report line stay attributed possibilities.
