This note is NP NOW. The stamp is developing, because the missing count can change. It was rechecked at about 9:44 am IST on 9 October 2026 and posted at 9:52 am IST. The figures below are as Nepal's authorities gave them on 5 October. They were unchanged in an AP report on 7 October, and unchanged at this recheck.

Nepal ended the search and rescue operation for the 26 August flash flood on the Bhotekoshi and Trishuli rivers. The decision was on Monday, 5 October, forty days after the flood. The National Council for Disaster Risk Reduction and Management, chaired by Prime Minister Balendra Shah, took it, and the work is shifting to reconstruction and rehabilitation. Ending that operation is not the same as stopping every rescue. Officials told Reuters that emergency rescues will still be carried out if they are needed, and that remains or belongings found during rebuilding will be recovered.

The official toll, from Nepal's disaster authority via Reuters and The Times of India, is 1,455 dead, 5,285 still missing and 13,795 rescued. AP's 7 October report repeats 1,455 dead and 5,285 missing. These are the figures for people the authorities have recorded as dead, and for people still missing. This note does not say that everyone still missing has died.

Nepal's Department of Tourism lists 189 Indians among 606 missing foreign tourists from 36 countries, the largest group, The Times of India and Zee News report. That 189 is the tourism department's figure. It is not an MEA figure. Times Now, citing PTI, reports "over 180". Reuters does not give an Indian number. A state-wise list printed with the tourism-department figure adds up to more than 189. It is not used here.

The same meeting recommended NPR 559 billion, about ₹35,000 crore, for 504 rebuilding programmes, The Times of India and Zee News report.

Officials told Reuters they are considering a legal process, including how to declare missing people dead, so families can complete insurance, banking and other formalities. That is a process under consideration. It is not a declaration that the missing have died.

For families, the MEA opened a 24x7 control room on 27 August. The numbers, as the MEA issued them that day via DD India and The Times of India, are +91 11 2308 8718 and +91 11 2308 8719, WhatsApp +91 9968291988, and situationroom@mea.gov.in. The Embassy of India in Kathmandu gave +977 985 131 6807, +977 970 910 7500 and +977 981 032 6117, also on WhatsApp. No newer MEA statement, and no confirmation that the lines were still staffed, was found after 27 August.
