This note is NP EXPLAINS. The stamp is verified. The trade PDF from the Press Information Bureau was re-read at 2:53 pm IST on 16 October 2026, and the reel went up at 4:55 pm IST. The Hindu, The Indian Express and The Times of India report the same month. The figures are provisional.

Goods exports in September 2026 were $44.4 billion. That is 23.04 percent higher than a year earlier. Goods imports were $74.61 billion. The goods trade deficit, exports subtracted from imports, was $30.21 billion. The caption's plain definition is the gap that opens when a country buys more from abroad than it sells. That is the sense in which $30.21 billion is a deficit, not a surplus.

Two comparisons are both in the caption, and both can be true. The September 2026 deficit is smaller than the September 2025 deficit of $33 billion. It is larger than the August 2026 deficit of $26.86 billion. Narrower than last September is one sentence. Wider than this August is another. This note keeps both.

The sector lines come from the PIB PDF, re-read that afternoon, with the caption's round numbers beside them. Electronic goods exports were up 70.44 percent. The caption says electronics exports were up by about 70 percent. Petroleum product exports were up 31.90 percent. The caption says about 32 percent. Gold imports were down 81.27 percent. The caption says gold imports fell by about 81 percent. Those are the moves named. This note does not add sectors that the PDF line and the caption do not name.

Services exports logged what the caption and The Indian Express call a rare dip. The lines used here do not give a dollar figure for that dip, so this note does not invent one.

A separate figure of $500 billion is the Commerce Secretary's conditional hope. The caption says it is not a fixed target. This note does not turn that hope into a promise, and it does not spell out a condition the caption does not spell out.

The caption's reason for the attention is that exports support factory jobs and earn dollars for imports such as oil. That is the caption's line. It is not a forecast for October.

The moving pictures are representational. One clip is by Marian Croitoru on Pexels. Another is aerial footage of a container terminal at the Port of Cork, in Ireland, by FILMING CORK, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 3.0. The credit says it is not an Indian port. The data cards are drawn by Neutral Politics from the PIB PDF, with the kicker "Courtesy: PIB". The credit for the graphics is "NP graphic".

What is verified is the September goods arithmetic and the named sector moves. What is provisional is the release itself. What is a hope, and not a target, is the $500 billion figure.
