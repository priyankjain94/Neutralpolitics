This note is NP NOW. The stamp is verified for the detention. It is not a finding on the allegations. The carousel was posted at 4:43 pm IST on 17 October 2026. A recheck at 4:37 pm IST found no formal arrest and no public statement from the Enforcement Directorate. The caption's line that an arrest was not confirmed is timed at 4:35 pm IST. The stamp basis had recorded the same gap as of 3:05 pm IST. News18, in updates the package places at 1:37 pm and 2:28 pm IST, says a formal arrest is being considered. IANS at 3:31 pm IST, and the package's note on Bhaskar English, Amar Ujala and Live Hindustan, say detained or questioned, with arrest under consideration. Those words are not an arrest.

What the reports agree on is the stop. Hindustan Times, citing ED sources, says film producer Amardeep Singh Grewal was detained at Delhi airport and was not arrested, according to those sources. News18 says he was taken into custody at Indira Gandhi International airport and brought to the ED headquarters in Delhi for questioning. Aaj Tak says he was detained, with no official confirmation of an arrest, and no response from Grewal. IANS, in a post by Arvind Gunasekar citing ED sources at 1:47 pm IST, says he was stopped at the airport through a look-out circular and was being questioned. ABP Sanjha says official confirmation from the ED was still awaited.

The caption's sequence of the summons is this. The ED called him on 7 October. He asked for an adjournment. There was a summons on 9 October. He did not appear. A look-out circular followed. Hindustan Times confirms the 7 October adjournment, the missed appearance on 9 October, and the circular. The Times of India, in its 7 October report, confirms the summons that day, the adjournment, and a fresh summons for 9 October.

A look-out circular, as the caption explains it, is an alert sent to airports and border posts so that a person an agency wants to question can be stopped or flagged while travelling. That is the caption's definition, not a court ruling in this note.

The case, in every report the caption cites, is an alleged ₹5-crore bribery case. The reports do not describe it the same way, and this note does not choose. News18 and ABP Sanjha say the case is linked to transfers and postings. Hindustan Times and The Times of India say there was an alleged demand linked to settling an Amritsar rape-case FIR. Both versions stay attributed. Dr Gurpreet Kaur's name appears in the case, the caption says. She is the wife of Punjab Chief Minister Bhagwant Mann. The Times of India reports her statement that the allegations are baseless, fabricated and politically motivated. This note does not describe her as an accused, and it does not say she took money. No amount recovered is reported. The ED has not put out a public statement, the caption says.

ABP Sanjha alone says Grewal was arriving from Canada. The package marks that as a single source.

On who he is to the Chief Minister, the accounts conflict, and this note keeps them apart. The Aam Aadmi Party and the Punjab government say Grewal is not the Chief Minister's relative. A Hindustan Times report on the missed 9 October summons says the Aam Aadmi Party says there is no association with the Chief Minister or his family. Some reports call him a relative or a close aide. That is not confirmed. This note does not state a family relation as fact. No response from Grewal is reported. The caption's "why it matters" line says the probe reaches a name linked in reports to a sitting Chief Minister's family, that there are only allegations, and that a court has decided nothing. That is the caption's frame. It is not a finding of this note.
