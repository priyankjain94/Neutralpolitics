This is a check of a court order, not a new report of a crime. A Delhi Additional Sessions Judge, sitting as a POCSO court, ended a sentencing order of 11 July 2026 with a letter to the child survivor. The Delhi High Court returned to that letter in an order of 22 September 2026, in criminal appeal CRL.A. 849/2026. The notes used here say the letter was widely reported from 28 to 30 September. Neutral Politics is using the High Court order, the PDF of which is carried via LiveLaw, BBC News dated 30 September, and Hindustan Times dated 29 September, timed at 4:49 pm IST. This note was posted at 9:58 pm IST on 1 October 2026. It does not name or describe the child, the family, a locality, or the person who was sentenced.

The letter, as the High Court order reproduces it and as BBC News and Hindustan Times report it, includes these lines: "Beta, this Court heard you." And: "Now, beta, live your life. Go to school. Make friends. Laugh. Play. Dream." Those are the lines in the material used here. This note does not reprint the rest of the letter.

The sentence recorded on 11 July is rigorous imprisonment for life, meaning the remainder of the person's natural life, with a fine of ₹1 lakh. The order also records compensation of ₹10.5 lakh to the child. The appeal against that sentence is pending. A pending appeal means the High Court has not yet decided whether the sentence stands for good.

On 22 September, Justices Prathiba M. Singh and Dinesh Bhatt called the letter "a message of encouragement." They directed the District Legal Services Authority to print the letter and hand it to the survivor or the family, together with the compensation, within two weeks. They declined to suspend the sentence at this stage. A refusal to suspend means the life sentence is not paused while this stage of the appeal is where the order left it. The direction to hand over the letter is an order about the letter and the compensation. It is not a new sentence, and it is not an acquittal.

The same order lists what comes next. On 4 November 2026 the District Legal Services Authority is to file a status report on the compensation. On 20 January 2027 the appeal is listed. This check does not have the status report, because that date has not arrived in the material used here. It also does not have a judgment on the appeal.

What is not known from these sources is whether the printed letter has been handed over, what the 4 November report will say, and how the appeal is decided. Those gaps stay open. Nothing here is a fresh allegation about how the case arose. The facts used are the sentence, the letter as reproduced, the High Court's directions, and the two listing dates.

If a child needs help in India, the Child Helpline is 1098. It is available 24 hours, under the Ministry of Women and Child Development.

## What's next

The dates on the High Court order are 4 November 2026, for the legal-services status report on the compensation, and 20 January 2027, for the appeal. Until those, the recorded position is the one in the 22 September order: the letter is to be printed and handed over with the compensation, and the life sentence is not suspended at this stage.
