On Monday, 5 October 2026, a Supreme Court bench of Chief Justice Surya Kant and Justices Joymalya Bagchi and V. Mohana refused an interim, ex-parte order suspending Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar. LiveLaw, Hindustan Times with PTI, Bar & Bench, The Economic Times and NDTV reported that refusal. This note is NP NOW. What the court did, as those reports give it, is verified. The petitioners' allegations are not.

The court issued notice to the Union, the Election Commission of India, the Chief Election Commissioner, Election Commissioners Sukhbir Singh Sandhu and Vivek Joshi, Senior Deputy Election Commissioner Maneesh Garg and Director General (Information Technology) Seema Khanna. Replies were sought in about a week. Bar & Bench quotes the Chief Justice as saying to wait for 10 days. This note keeps the period as about a week, which is how the reports describe that wait. No next date of hearing was reported.

The Chief Justice said a newspaper report "may be 100% correct, partially correct or partially incorrect", and that the court cannot pass an order on that basis today. Bar & Bench and Indian Express reported those words. NDTV's account is the same limit: the court cannot pass an order based on a newspaper report, and it needs the poll body's response.

The bench also said what it can do later. If the records show that decisions were not unanimous, or were not by majority, the court can annul them. The pleas cite Section 18 of the 2023 Act on the Chief Election Commissioner and other Election Commissioners. That power is what the court described. It is not a finding that any decision failed that test.

On Form 6, the Chief Justice said the Supreme Court has not approved any modified form. Indian Express and India Today reported that remark. India Today quotes the line that a modified Form 6 "has not been approved by us".

What the petitioners allege stays in that category. Rakesh Kumar Singh and advocate Shailendra Mani Tripathi allege about 14 objections by the two Election Commissioners, from late 2025 to August 2026, on steps related to the Special Intensive Revision. Indian Express is among the reports of those pleas. The number is the petitioners' allegation. The court has not found it proved. This note does not treat it as a fact.

The Election Commission says its final decisions, including on the revision, were unanimous. The Economic Times reports that public line. It is the Commission's account. It is not the court's conclusion in this hearing.

Issuing notice is not a finding either way. It does not hold the Chief Election Commissioner at fault, and it does not dispose of the allegations. The Supreme Court upheld the validity of the Special Intensive Revision of electoral rolls in May 2026. The Tribune reported that ruling. It is a separate case. There was no stay of the revision on 5 October.

## What's next

Replies are sought in about a week. What the court does after those replies, and whether any decision is annulled, is not known from this hearing.
