## Update, 28 October

This page was updated at 9:37 am IST on 28 October 2026, when the new carousel was posted. The series is NP EXPLAINS. The stamp is VERIFIED, and it is marked as an update. Two lines sit at the top. The carousel follows IMD National Bulletin No. 24. A later IMD statement says the centre crossed the coast. That later statement is the latest line on the crossing. IMD bulletins change the numbers often. Follow IMD, the Andhra Pradesh State Disaster Management Authority (APSDMA) and the Odisha State Disaster Management Authority (OSDMA).

Bulletin No. 24 is numbered BOB/08/2026. It was issued at 08:45 IST on 28 October, from the position at 05:30 IST. At 05:30 IST, very severe cyclonic storm Arnab was near 16.4°N, 82.0°E. That is about 30 km east of Narsapur and about 45 km south-southwest of Yanam. For the previous three hours it was practically stationary. IMD said the landfall process was under way. The wall cloud on the northern side had reached land. The centre was still just off the coast, over the sea. IMD's wording, as the caption gives it, is that the storm is said to have crossed the coast when the centre comes over land. At this bulletin, IMD had not said the centre had crossed. A check at about 9:20 am IST found Bulletin No. 24 as the newest bulletin then online. The addresses checked for Bulletin No. 25 and Bulletin No. 26 were not online.

The forecast in Bulletin No. 24 was a very likely crossing between Bapatla and Visakhapatnam, close to the south of Yanam, with winds of 125-135 kmph gusting to 150 kmph. At landfall, IMD says a storm surge of 1 to 1.5 m is possible in low-lying areas. Reuters also reported that surge line. After the crossing, the forecast was weakening over land: a cyclonic storm by 11:30 IST, and a deep depression by 17:30 IST. Fishermen are advised not to go to sea until 29 October.

APSDMA says 84,396 people are in shelters in Andhra Pradesh. The caption says 21 flights and several trains have been cancelled. Reports differ, so this note gives no casualty count. Andhra Pradesh helplines are 112 and 1070.

The later IMD statement, from the package after that carousel, says Arnab crossed the Andhra Pradesh and Yanam coasts between 02:00 and 08:00 IST on 28 October, as a very severe cyclonic storm, with winds of 125-135 kmph gusting to 150 kmph. The crossing point was about 20 km east-northeast of Narsapur, about 40 km southwest of Yanam, and about 60 km southwest of Kakinada. By 08:30 IST it had weakened to a severe cyclonic storm, 110-120 kmph gusting to 135 kmph. IMD expects it to become a cyclonic storm and then a deep depression. Those next two stages are a forecast.

Chief Minister N. Chandrababu Naidu said no casualties had been reported so far due to the cyclone. That sentence is his. The New Indian Express and PTI are the reports named for it. This note does not turn his sentence into a count. The Kakinada superintendent of police says hundreds of trees were uprooted and the Uppada coastal road was washed away. That is the officer's account.

## Update, 27 October

The paragraphs under this heading are the 27 October update, from Bulletin No. 15. The 28 October lines above are the later line.

This page was updated at 8:50 am IST on 27 October 2026, when that carousel was posted. The series on this carousel is NP NOW. The stamp is VERIFIED, and it is marked as an update. As of IMD National Bulletin No. 15, numbered BOB/08/2026 and issued at 03:45 IST on 27 October, from the position at 02:30 IST: Cyclone Arnab is now a very severe cyclonic storm. IMD bulletins change the numbers often. The next bulletin can change them. Follow IMD, the Andhra Pradesh State Disaster Management Authority (APSDMA) and the Odisha State Disaster Management Authority (OSDMA).

At 02:30 IST on 27 October the storm was in the west-central Bay of Bengal, near 15.9°N, 85.4°E. That is about 300 km southeast of Visakhapatnam and about 360 km east-southeast of Kakinada. In the previous six hours it moved west-northwest at 25 kmph. IMD's RSMC Tropical Cyclone Advisory No. 7 says the system had intensified into a very severe cyclonic storm by 1800 UTC on 26 October, which is 23:30 IST. A check at about 8:45 am IST on 27 October found Bulletin No. 15 listed as the latest. The bulletin itself said the next message would be at 08:30 IST. Bulletin No. 16 was not online at that check.

IMD's forecast from Bulletin No. 15 is that the storm is likely to move west-northwest until the evening of 27 October, and then northwest. It is very likely to cross the Andhra Pradesh and Yanam coasts between Bapatla and Visakhapatnam, close to Yanam, as a very severe cyclonic storm, by the forenoon of 28 October. The wind line for that crossing is 115-125 kmph, gusting to 140 kmph. The crossing is that bulletin's forecast. As of that 27 October page, a crossing had not been reported, and the note did not set a clock time inside that forenoon.

At sea at 02:30 IST, the bulletin puts the winds at 125-135 kmph, gusting to 150 kmph. IMD says those winds may rise to 135-145 kmph, gusting to 160 kmph, by the afternoon of 27 October. That rise is a forecast. IMD also says a storm surge of 1.0-1.5 m is possible near Kakinada and Yanam at landfall. That surge line is IMD's wording for the time of landfall, and it is a forecast. Fishermen are advised not to go to sea on 27, 28 and 29 October.

Andhra Chief Minister N. Chandrababu Naidu has ordered that people be moved out of coastal and low-lying areas. The Hindu is the report named for that order. For 27 October, The Hindu reports a red alert for seven districts. The caption, and The Times of India, name Kakinada, Konaseema, East Godavari, West Godavari, Eluru and Yanam among the places under that alert. The caption lists Andhra Pradesh helplines 112 and 1070. The Hindu is also named for the helplines. This update does not give a count of people moved. It does not give a damage figure or a casualty count.

IMD's categories, as printed in the track legend with knots converted to kmph, are these. A cyclonic storm is 62-88 kmph. A severe cyclonic storm is 89-117 kmph. A very severe cyclonic storm is 118-166 kmph. The caption says a red alert means "take action".

## Background: Bulletin No. 2, 25 October

The paragraphs under this heading are the note as published on 25 October, from Bulletin No. 2. Bulletin No. 15, at the top of this page, is the latest line. Bulletin No. 8 is the 26 October update.

This note is verified. The series is NP EXPLAINS. The carousel was posted at 8:58 am IST on 25 October 2026. The stamp is VERIFIED. The cover is an NP graphic, and the data are credited to IMD. No IMD image is used. The line that sits with every figure below is this: as of IMD Bulletin No. 2, 02:00 IST on 25 October; forecasts may change.

IMD National Bulletin No. 2 is numbered BOB/08/2026. It was issued at 02:00 IST on 25 October, from observations at 23:30 IST on 24 October. The caption, and the bulletin as this package uses it, say the low over the Bay of Bengal became a depression at 17:30 IST on 24 October. The brief places that depression over the east-central and adjoining southeast Bay of Bengal. It is a depression. A cyclone had not formed when this bulletin was issued. The bulletin does not give the depression a name, and this note does not add one.

The forecast in that bulletin, which the caption sets out, runs in four steps. IMD expects a deep depression on 25 October. It expects a cyclonic storm by the morning of 26 October. It expects a severe cyclonic storm on 27 October. It expects a very severe cyclonic storm near the Andhra coast by the morning of 28 October. Each of those lines is a forecast of a later stage. None of them is a report that the later stage has already happened.

On the coast, IMD says the system is likely to cross the Andhra Pradesh and Yanam coasts between Bapatla and Visakhapatnam by the forenoon of 28 October. The wind line in the same forecast is 115-125 kmph, gusting to 140 kmph. The caption says this is a forecast and that the track can change. This note does not pick one town inside that stretch, and it does not set a clock time inside that forenoon. The Hindu is named as a report of this forecast. The forecast itself is IMD's.

The alert with the forecast is a Pre-Cyclone Watch, a Yellow Message, for the Andhra Pradesh and adjoining south Odisha coasts. For rain, the forecast is isolated extremely heavy rain over coastal Andhra Pradesh and Yanam on 27 and 28 October.

The fishermen's line, attributed to IMD, is not to venture into the west-central Bay on 26, 27 and 28 October, and not to venture along and off the Andhra Pradesh and Odisha coasts on 27 and 28 October. The caption adds that people should follow official alerts from IMD, from the Andhra Pradesh State Disaster Management Authority (APSDMA) and from the Odisha State Disaster Management Authority (OSDMA), and the advice of the local administration.

This page is an update. The package note says it updates the 24 October 08:38 package, which forecast only a cyclonic storm near north Andhra and south Odisha. The sources named for this bulletin are IMD National Bulletin No. 2, an IMD press release of 24 October, and The Hindu. The brief also names an earlier Hindu report with that earlier forecast. A check of mausam.imd.gov.in and the RSMC New Delhi page at about 9:00 am IST on 25 October found no newer bulletin. The latest tropical weather outlook (TWO) listing was 25 October at 02:20 IST.

Read together, the lines that stay inside the bulletin are these. The system is a depression, formed at 17:30 IST on 24 October over the east-central and adjoining southeast Bay of Bengal. The forecast steps are a deep depression on 25 October, a cyclonic storm by the morning of 26 October, a severe cyclonic storm on 27 October, and a very severe cyclonic storm near the Andhra coast by the morning of 28 October. The likely crossing is the Andhra Pradesh and Yanam coasts between Bapatla and Visakhapatnam by the forenoon of 28 October, with winds of 115-125 kmph gusting to 140 kmph. Fishermen are advised to stay out of the west-central Bay on 26-28 October and out of the waters along and off the Andhra Pradesh and Odisha coasts on 27-28 October. This note does not give a damage figure or a casualty count. It stops at the forecast.
