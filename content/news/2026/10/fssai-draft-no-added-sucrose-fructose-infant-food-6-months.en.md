This note is NP EXPLAINS. The stamp is verified. The Gazette of India text, number CG-DL-E-16102026-277221, was read through Gazette Tracker at 4:57 pm IST on 17 October 2026. BusinessLine, published on 17 October, and Mint were read at the same time. The reel was posted at 6:20 pm IST. The caption's hashtag is NPExplains.

The document is a draft amendment to the Food Safety and Standards (Foods for Infant Nutrition) Regulations, 2020. It is dated at New Delhi on 15 October 2026 and appeared in the Gazette of India on 16 October 2026. It is signed by Rajit Punhani, chief executive officer of the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India. The stamp basis places the operative proviso in regulation 3(6).

The gazette sentence, quoted as read, is: "Sucrose and fructose shall not be added in the products intended for infants up to the age of 6 months." The caption's gloss is that sucrose and fructose would not be added to products made for infants up to six months. BusinessLine reports the same proposed restriction and the same quote. Mint reports the quote by way of the health ministry.

The draft gives sixty days for objections and suggestions. Mint says the final regulations come after review. The health ministry is the reviewer named there. Until that review is done, this text is a proposal. It is not in force. The final rule may change. The caption says the same, and adds that parents and companies are the people the rule would matter to, if it is adopted.

The limit of the sentence is narrow, and this note keeps it narrow. It is about sucrose and fructose that are added. It is about products intended for infants up to the age of six months. It is not a line about every sugar, and it is not a line about older children. No brand name is in the sources used here, and none is added.

The aim, as the health ministry and FSSAI describe it in the reports, is to reduce infants' early exposure to added sugars, because the first six months matter for growth. BusinessLine reports that aim as well. That is their stated reason for the draft. It is not a finding, in this note, that any product now on sale fails a test.

The caption also says that current rules allow these sugars to be added within limits. The source file says that sentence comes from the brief, and that the 2020 principal regulation was not read again for this post. Outlets frame the draft as a tightening of existing rules. This note does not present the "within limits" line as a fresh reading of the 2020 text. It remains the caption's description of the present rules.

What readers should not take from the reel is a ban already in force, a safety verdict on a named product, or a rule that reaches past six months. The caption's own caution is the working line: draft only, 60 days for objections, the final rule may change, and the proposal is only about added sucrose and fructose in products for infants up to six months.
