This note is verified. It is not a launch report. ISRO's release of 16 October 2026 was read in full at 9:03 am IST on 17 October, together with the ISRO Gaganyaan page. The Hindu and The Times of India, both of 16 October, were read in full at the same check. The reel was posted at about 9:25 am IST on 18 October. The hashtags on the reel are ISRO and Gaganyaan.

ISRO said a series of Crew Module–Service Module separation tests has been completed. The work was done on a ground test set-up at the Vikram Sarabhai Space Centre, VSSC, in Thiruvananthapuram. The set-up simulated the Crew Module and Service Module joined as an assembly. This was a ground test. It was not a flight. It was not a launch.

The release describes what the set-up did. Umbilical connections between the Crew Module and the Service Module were severed. Before re-entry, separation is carried out by sequenced pyrotechnic devices and jettisoning systems. ISRO said the tests validated the design adequacy of that separation.

The same release lists what was checked for shock survivability: avionics, INS, the aft and conical heat-shield TPS, the deployable umbilical shield, and the CMUS and THCS pyros. The release does not expand INS, TPS, CMUS, or THCS, and this note does not add expansions. The caption glosses the check as avionics, navigation systems, and heat shields that withstood the shock. That gloss is the caption's. The list above is the release's.

The two modules have different jobs, in the pages read for this note. The Times of India says the Crew Module houses the crew. The caption calls the Crew Module the capsule astronauts will sit in. The ISRO Gaganyaan page says the Service Module supports the Crew Module in orbit, and that it contains thermal, propulsion, power, and avionics systems. The Times of India says the Service Module supports the spacecraft, and that the separation comes before re-entry. The Hindu reports the same test facts, and its wording for the next mission is below.

The launch that is still ahead is not this test. The ISRO release says the first uncrewed Gaganyaan mission, G1, is targeted for launch in 2026. The Hindu says G1 is targeted this year. In a story dated 2026, "this year" and "2026" can be read as the same year, but the two phrases are kept as each outlet wrote them. Neither line says G1 has launched.

The picture and the moving footage are ISRO's. The footage comes from the video on the release page, with the title card skipped. The stills are the release's photographs of the separation test set-up before the test and after it. The credit is Courtesy: ISRO. The package notes that no explicit licence text was checked on that material. The reel's music is Metriko, "Somber Constellations Ambient", from Pixabay. The package says that track does not require attribution.

The caption calls Gaganyaan India's human spaceflight programme, and says a clean separation of the Crew Module from the Service Module, at the right time, matters for a safe return. That "why it matters" line is the caption's framing of the release.

What was completed is a series of ground separation tests at VSSC. What was not done is a launch or a flight. What ISRO still names as a target is an uncrewed G1 launch in 2026. The Hindu's phrase is that G1 is targeted this year.
