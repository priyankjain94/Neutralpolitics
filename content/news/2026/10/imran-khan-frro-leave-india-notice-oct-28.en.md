This note is NP NOW. The leave-India notice is verified. The police position that foreigners cannot protest is marked as a claim. The Bombay High Court challenge is developing. The note was rechecked at 7:47 am IST on 16 October 2026, and the post went up at 7:56 am IST.

The Foreigners Regional Registration Office (FRRO) in Mumbai has served actor Imran Khan a notice to leave India. He is to depart on or before 28 October 2026. The Hindu, The Indian Express and Mint report the notice and that date. He is a US citizen and an OCI cardholder. Reports also give his name as Imran Khan Pal.

An OCI card is an Overseas Citizen of India card. It is not Indian citizenship. A person who holds one remains a foreign national. A leave-India notice and the cancellation of an OCI card are different processes. Cancellation of an OCI card sits under Section 7D of the Citizenship Act. This note is about the leave-India notice.

The notice follows a protest on 10 October 2026 at August Kranti Maidan in Mumbai. The protest was against Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar. Imran Khan joined it. Police briefly detained him there. The reports describe that brief detention. They do not describe an arrest.

The path to the notice, as The Indian Express reports it, starts with a complaint by former BJP MP Kirit Somaiya. Gamdevi police forwarded that complaint to the FRRO. The FRRO in Mumbai then served the notice.

The law cited on the notice is the Immigration and Foreigners Act, 2025. The sections cited are Section 7 and Section 29. The Indian Express, Mint, and Hindustan Times, reporting the notice text, carry those citations. This note does not paraphrase the sections beyond what those reports use them for: they are the provisions the notice relies on.

The police position is that foreigners cannot take part in a protest. That position is contested, and this note marks it as a claim. The Indian Express and Mint report that the Immigration and Foreigners Act, 2025, does not expressly list protest. The Act, on those reports, does not itself name protest as a barred act. The police reading and the text those outlets describe are not the same thing. This note does not adopt the police position as a finding.

What happens in court is a separate question, and it is open. Lawyers are moving to challenge the notice in the Bombay High Court. The Indian Express, in a report timestamped 2:53 am IST on 16 October, describes a process of filing. Hindustan Times reports that the move is to be made on 16 October. The Hindu likewise reports that he is to move the High Court against the notice. The New Indian Express is the only outlet that wrote that he has moved or approached the court. Because that firmer line is in one report, this note keeps the softer wording used on the post: he is moving to challenge the notice. The filing is developing. No outcome is known. A later report that the petition has been registered, or a court order, would be the next verified step. Until then, the challenge stays developing.
