This note is NP NOW. The stamp is verified for three records: Trump's announcement, US Treasury General License 135, and the Kremlin's statement of readiness. The tonnage, and the line that prices will come down, are Trump's claims. It was last checked at 11:22 am IST on 10 October 2026 and posted at 11:22 am IST.

On 9 October, US President Donald Trump and Russian President Vladimir Putin spoke by phone. Trump then announced a Russian diesel deal on Truth Social. He says Putin agreed to supply diesel to the United States and to global markets. The reports listed here do not describe a paper the two sides put their names to. This note says that he announces the deal, and that Trump says Putin agreed.

The tonnage is Trump's claim. He claims more than 300,000 tons immediately, 500,000 tons in November, 1 million tons after that, and up to 3 million tons more. Those figures are not described as cargo already delivered. The Kremlin's readout does not confirm any of them. A claim of a future cargo is not a delivery.

What the Kremlin did confirm is readiness. The line given for that readout is that Russia is "ready to supply oil and oil products to American and global markets." Readiness is not a quantity. No amount is attached to that sentence. The Kremlin confirmed readiness. It did not confirm 300,000 tons, or 500,000 tons, or 1 million tons, or the further 3 million.

The mechanism on the US side is a licence. The Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control issued General License 135. It allows the sale and import of Russian diesel through 12:01 a.m. EDT on 7 April 2027. It excludes debits to accounts of the Russian central bank, of NWF and of MinFin. The licence is temporary, and it is about diesel. Sanctions on Russia are not ended. A window for one product is not a repeal of the wider regime.

Trump also claims that prices will come down. That sentence is his claim. It is not a figure from the Kremlin, and it is not a finding written into the licence. This note leaves it labelled as Trump's claim.

The post places the licence next to a separate line it records: a 25 percent tariff the United States had applied to India over Russian oil. The four news reports listed for this note do not describe that tariff as lifted. The licence text summarised here is about Russian diesel imports under General License 135. It is not a line about India's tariff.

The news reports are CNBC, AP as carried by NPR, Politico, and RFE/RL. They are the links under this article. The licence terms and the Kremlin sentence are the records the stamp treats as verified. The tonnage and the price sentence stay labelled as Trump's claims. Where a number appears above, it appears as his claim, not as a Kremlin confirmation.

The cover uses two public-domain photographs from the Anchorage meeting on 15 August 2025. One is from the White House. The other is by Benjamin D. Applebaum for DVIDS. Both are shown whole.

Read together, the sequence in this note is short. A phone call on 9 October. An announcement on Truth Social. A claim, from Trump, of volumes and of lower prices. A Kremlin sentence of readiness with no amounts. A US licence that runs to 12:01 a.m. EDT on 7 April 2027, bars those named debits, and leaves the wider sanctions in place.
