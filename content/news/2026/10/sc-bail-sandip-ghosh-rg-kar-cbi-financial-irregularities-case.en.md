This note is NP EXPLAINS. The stamp is verified. The Supreme Court order in SLP (Crl.) 16465/2026, Sandip Ghosh v. Central Bureau of Investigation, dated 15 October 2026, was read in full at 4:56 pm IST on 17 October 2026. It is signed by Justices M.M. Sundresh and Prasanna B. Varale. LiveLaw, timed 6:17 pm IST on 16 October, and The Times of India were read at the same check. The carousel was posted at 6:44 pm IST on 17 October. The package's as-of line is 4:58 pm IST.

The order grants bail. It sets aside the Calcutta High Court order of 23 February 2026, which had rejected bail. The terms and conditions are for the trial court to set. The caption says the court looked at the length of custody and the stage of the trial. The order, as the stamp basis records it, notes more than two years of incarceration, and that 7 of 147 witnesses have been examined. LiveLaw and The Times of India match those two figures.

The case this order is about is a CBI case. The allegations, in the order's own framing, are financial irregularities and manipulation of merit scores. Both are allegations. This note does not treat them as proved. The sections in the order are Indian Penal Code section 120-B read with sections 409, 420, 468 and 471, and Prevention of Corruption Act section 7 and section 13(2) read with section 13(1)(a). The caption says those sections are in the chargesheet.

This order is not the RG Kar rape and murder case. The caption says that in those words, and this note repeats it because the college's name is shared and the cases are not. A reader who knows the college from the rape and murder case is reading a different prosecution here: the CBI case on alleged financial irregularities and alleged merit-score manipulation.

Bail is not an acquittal. The caption says the charges remain and the CBI trial continues. Whether he has been released from jail is not confirmed. The sources marked unused include "release from jail (not confirmed)." A single IANS wire, that the victim's father opposes bail, was not used, and this note does not carry it.

The order is dated 15 October 2026. LiveLaw reported it on 16 October at 6:17 pm IST. The Times of India also reported the bail, the custody of more than two years, the 7 of 147 witnesses, and a quote from the order. The PDF used here is the copy LiveLaw carries.

The photograph is a file picture of the Supreme Court of India, by Subhashish Panigrahi, Wikimedia Commons, file Supreme Court of India 01, licence CC BY-SA 4.0. It is not a picture of the accused, and it is not a picture from the hearing.

What the order does is grant bail and set aside the High Court's rejection, with terms left to the trial court. What it does not do is acquit, end the trial, or decide the rape and murder case. What is still open is whether he has been released.
