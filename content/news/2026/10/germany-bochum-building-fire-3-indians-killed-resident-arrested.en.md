This note is NP NOW. The stamp is verified. It was checked at 8:10 am IST on 15 October 2026, rechecked at 10:15 am IST, and posted at 10:17 am IST. This file is a draft.

A fire broke out in a residential building in Bochum, in North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany, on 13 October, at about 4:15 in the morning, local time. That clock time is the local time in the reports. This note does not convert it.

Three Indian nationals were killed. They were two women, aged 25 and 27, and one man, aged 26. Five people were injured. Süddeutsche Zeitung, carrying dpa, WDR, The Hindu and Anadolu place the city, the fire and those deaths. Anadolu reports that German police identified the three dead as Indian nationals.

German police arrested a 41-year-old resident of the same building on suspicion of murder. A judge ordered that he be held in custody. dpa reported the arrest and the custody order on 14 October. The suspicion is the basis of the arrest, as those reports give it. An arrest is not a verdict. The motive is not known.

The recheck at 10:15 am IST on 15 October read WDR, Radio Bochum, a dpa report via t-online timed 4:00 on 15 October, Der Spiegel, The Indian Express and The Hindu. The account was unchanged: three Indians dead, and the 41-year-old resident held in custody. The recheck describes that custody as being on a murder and arson warrant. This note records that description from the recheck. It does not turn the warrant into a finding about how the fire started. Radio Bochum, t-online, Der Spiegel and The Indian Express are named in that recheck. They are not given as separate links here.

The Embassy of India in Berlin said, in a line quoted via ANI by The Tribune, Gulf News and Republic: "All 3 were studying in Germany." That sentence is the embassy's. This note uses those words as the embassy's quote, and not as its own description.

The Hindu and Manorama report that the three were from Kerala. This note does not name them. Ages and the count are the identifying facts it uses.

The embassy in Berlin and the consulate in Frankfurt are helping the families. The government of Kerala is working on repatriation of the bodies. The Hindu reports that repatriation work.

The cover is a graphic made by Neutral Politics, from NP-made maps and icons. The maps use Natural Earth, which is public domain. The on-screen credit is "NP graphic". There is no photograph of anyone who died, and no broadcast clip.

What is verified is the place and the local time, the three deaths and the five injuries, the arrest on suspicion, the judge's custody order, the embassy sentence, Kerala as the origin The Hindu and Manorama report, and the help being given to the families. What is not a verdict is the suspicion itself. The motive remains unreported. A later charge sheet, or a court finding, would be a different record from this arrest.
