This note is NP NOW. The stamp is verified. The primary posts are Ambassador Vinay Mohan Kwatra's on 15 October and Congresswoman Pramila Jayapal's. The Hindu, Hindustan Times and The Times of India were live-checked, and the quotes were rechecked at 5:10 pm IST on 16 October against The Hindu. The post went up at 5:15 pm IST.

Jayapal, identified in the caption as a Democrat from Washington state, wrote on X: "I am deeply concerned about the actions of the Indian government to purge 130 million people from the voting rolls. This appears to be a similar playbook to what the Trump Administration is doing here at home to undermine voting rights in a democracy." She also wrote that she would "stand with the Indian people who are engaging in non-violent protests".

Kwatra's reply, on X on 15 October, is this: "The remarks by @RepJayapal on India's electoral and political processes are unacceptable interference in India's internal affairs. India is a vibrant democracy, with robust, independent constitutional institutions and a longstanding record of free and fair elections. Always open to constructive conversation with the Congresswoman to dispel her misconceptions." The Hindu's rendering drops a comma after "robust". The wording here is the primary post, which Hindustan Times also carries. He said he had addressed misconceptions. He called the remarks unacceptable interference. That is the verb in the post.

The number 130 million, which the caption also writes as 13 crore, is Jayapal's figure. The caption also calls it the opposition's figure. It is not, in these lines, an Election Commission total. The Times of India, citing Election Commission data, gives a different pair of counts. In phases 1 and 2 of the special intensive revision, about 72 million entries were removed from draft rolls across 13 states and Union Territories. In an ongoing phase 3, across 18 states and Union Territories, 61.6 million electors have been excluded so far. Hindustan Times reports that the Commission does not treat the whole figure as confirmed deletions, and that eligible voters left out can apply through claims and objections and through Form 6. This note sets the posts and those counts side by side. It does not decide the revision.

The caption's "why now" is that protests over the revision and over Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar are continuing, and that the Supreme Court has issued notice on INDIA bloc petitions. That notice is a separate published note: [SC notice to the EC on the SIR plea](/news/2026/10/sc-notice-eci-india-bloc-sir-plea-delhi-protest-cctv-home-secretary-apology). It is not this exchange.

The caption's practical line is that a person votes if their name is on the roll, and that readers should check their own entry. It also says this is one US lawmaker's statement, not an official stand of the United States government.

The photographs are public domain, via Wikimedia Commons. Jayapal's is an official portrait by the U.S. House Office of Photography. Kwatra's is a U.S. Department of State photograph. The other cards are graphics made by Neutral Politics. The credit for those is "NP graphic".

What is verified is the two posts and the way the Indian papers report the Commission's counts. What this note does not do is judge the revision, or treat one lawmaker's post as the United States government's position.
