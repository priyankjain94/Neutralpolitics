This note is a claim check, and the claim is unverified. The carousel was posted at 5:49 pm IST on 20 October 2026. It is held as a draft because the underlying post is an unverified hacker claim, not a confirmed incident. The sources are India Today, The Economic Times, Moneycontrol and the Free Press Journal, plus the caption's account of a post on X.

The claim, as the caption sets it out, is this. A group called Siriusim says it has material linked to the State Bank of India: about 3.75 lakh rows in CSV form, and 2,722 PDF files. The claim, as described, includes loan documents and account-opening documents. CyberPulse56, an account on X, flagged the claim on 16 October and itself called the material unverified. India Today reports that same flag and the same two figures. As of the reports used here, nothing in the claim has been confirmed.

India Today, The Economic Times and Moneycontrol say SBI had not answered the questions put to it. That is the status those reports give: no reply in hand. A check described in the caption, of SBI's official account on X and of the news, had not found a statement from SBI, from the Reserve Bank of India, or from CERT-In. The caption times that check as still open when the carousel was prepared. This note uses 5:48 pm IST on 20 October, the time given for that check.

Where the rows came from is not known. The caption lists the open possibilities as SBI itself, a subsidiary, a vendor, or an older leak. Those are possibilities, not findings.

The number also does not mean a number of people. The claim is a count of CSV rows. Moneycontrol says the number of entries does not, by itself, show how many individual customers are affected. Headline wording is not the same as that claim. India Today's headline speaks of users. The Economic Times headline speaks of accounts. The Free Press Journal's headline also frames the material as customer data. Those are the outlets' frames. The figure in the claim, as the caption records it, remains rows and files.

Moneycontrol, in the caption's account of that report, says banks must report a cyber incident to the RBI within six hours and must also inform CERT-In. That is a rule the report states. It is not a finding that a report was or was not filed in this matter. No such filing is in the material used here.

The caption's safety lines, for anyone who banks with SBI or anyone else, are separate from the claim. Do not share an OTP, a PIN or a password. A bank does not ask for those. Treat with care a call, an SMS or a link that tries to win trust by quoting a loan or KYC details. If money has moved, call 1930 or report it at cybercrime.gov.in. The caption says the desk will update this if SBI, the RBI or CERT-In issues a statement.

The graphic is one NP made. The caption says no sample data is shown.
