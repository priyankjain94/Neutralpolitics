This note is two tracks. They are not one event. It is NP NOW. The stamp is developing, because allegations in both tracks are not adjudicated. It was posted at 4:21 pm IST on 4 October 2026.

On Sunday, 4 October, Uddhav Thackeray of Shiv Sena (UBT) and Raj Thackeray of the Maharashtra Navnirman Sena led a joint march in Mumbai. It went from Metro Cinema to the Brihanmumbai Municipal Corporation headquarters. The demand was the removal of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar over alleged irregularities in the Special Intensive Revision of electoral rolls. State Congress leaders and leaders of the NCP (Sharad Pawar) took part. Hindustan Times, India Today, and PTI with The Hindu reported that march. India Today is timed at 12:56 pm IST. The Hindu with PTI is timed at 12:57 pm IST. The Hindustan Times live file is timed at 12:54 pm IST. An earlier Hindustan Times report was updated at 10:08 am IST.

Only the tricolour was allowed. Indian Express reported that limit: no party flags. The route was changed from Byculla after a police request. Hindustan Times and Mid-day reported the change. Mid-day and Indian Express are dated 3 October, before the march, on the route and the flags. NDTV with IANS, and ANI via LatestLY at 11:50 am IST, reported beforehand that the brothers would lead the protest. The march as it happened is the later reports above.

What the speakers demanded, and what they alleged, is not a finding. Raj Thackeray called for the Special Intensive Revision to be cancelled, and for ballot papers. India Today and Hindustan Times reported that call. Uddhav Thackeray alleged an unfair appointment of the Chief Election Commissioner. The Hindustan Times live file and India Today reported that allegation. The irregularities are alleged. The appointment claim is alleged. Neither is adjudicated here.

Reports called the turnout large. No agreed crowd figure was given, so this note does not print one. No response from the Election Commission to the march was found in the sources checked.

The second track is Delhi, and it is separate. Delhi Police said on X on 4 October that three women journalists alleged sexual harassment by police officers during the 3 October protest at Jantar Mantar. The police said the complaints were transferred to the Crime Branch for a fair and impartial enquiry. Hindustan Times, timed at 11:54 am IST, and ETV Bharat, timed at 12:35 pm IST, reported that post. ETV Bharat embeds the police post. Police confirmed that the complaints were made. They did not confirm the allegations.

One journalist alleged in a video that a senior officer groped and pushed her while she filmed. ETV Bharat and The Tribune reported that allegation. The Tribune is timed at 5:51 am IST. It is an allegation. It is not a finding. This note does not name the complainants. It does not name the officers.

No FIR was reported as of The Hindu's update on 4 October. That live file, in an update timed at 9:53 am IST, said Delhi Police had yet to file an FIR. The transfer to the Crime Branch is not itself an FIR.

Journalists protested outside Parliament Street police station, seeking FIRs. Rahul Gandhi demanded an FIR. The Press Club of India urged action. Times Now, timed at 8:17 am IST, is among the reports of the journalists' protest. A complaint is not a finding. The allegations remain unadjudicated.

Neutral Politics checked these outlets on 4 October 2026 (IST). The check behind the note was about 1:45 pm IST. The note was posted at 4:21 pm IST. At posting, the material still showed no FIR, and no Election Commission response to the Mumbai march.

## Update, 5 October 2026

This update is Viral or Verified number 2, under NP VERIFY. It was posted at 2:15 pm IST on 5 October. It is about a viral image. It is not a finding on the complaints.

A social media post in the row over the women journalists' complaints carried a purported "CCTV" image. Delhi Police said on X on 4 October, at about 3:11 pm IST: "The post contains AI-generated/manipulated visuals. The CCTV footage image is fake." The police advised citizens not to circulate such content, and warned that sharing such posts may attract legal action. Delhi Police also described the viral post as carrying unverified claims presented as facts. PIB Fact Check reposted the police alert the same day, at about 3:37 pm IST, as an "AI-generated content alert by Delhi Police". Bharat Express reported that PIB warning. A Delhi Police DCP told Times Now that the footage "has no relation with the on-ground footage of the concerned day". Times Now's report of that comment is timed at 7:35 pm IST on 4 October.

The misleading label applies only to that image. It rests on the Delhi Police post and the PIB Fact Check repost. Neutral Politics has not independently analysed the image. The image is not shown here. The graphic below is a Neutral Politics schematic. It is not the viral image, and it is not a photograph of any person.

A fake image does not prove or disprove any complaint. The alleged complaints stay where the earlier part of this note left them. Hindustan Times, quoting Delhi Police on X, reports that three women journalists made complaints and that they were sent to the Crime Branch for a "fair and impartial enquiry". That Hindustan Times account says New Delhi district made the transfer. Times Now reports that the Crime Branch is verifying actual CCTV and body-camera recordings. No FIR had been reported as of 4 October, in Times Now, The Tribune and The Hindu. As of the morning of 5 October, a check of The Hindu and Indian Express still found no FIR registered. No complainant is named here. No officer is named here.

## What's next

Whether the Crime Branch inquiry leads to an FIR, and whether the Election Commission answers the Mumbai march, is not in these reports. The 5 October update settles only what Delhi Police and PIB Fact Check said about the viral image. It does not settle the complaints.
