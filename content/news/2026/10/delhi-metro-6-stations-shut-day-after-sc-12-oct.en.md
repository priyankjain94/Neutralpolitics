This note is NP NOW. The stamp is verified. It was re-checked at 12:55 pm IST on 12 October 2026, after a read at 12:52 pm IST, and posted at 1:05 pm IST. It records a time-bound closure on 12 October. The window DMRC announced ran from 6 am to 4 pm.

On 12 October the Delhi Metro Rail Corporation closed entry and exit at six Central Delhi stations. The stations were Patel Chowk, Janpath, Rajiv Chowk, Mandi House, Central Secretariat and Barakhamba Road. DMRC's post on X is the primary source. The reason it gave, in its own words, was "security reasons". It said the step was to be reviewed. Interchange at those stations stayed available. That is the advisory this note uses: a closed entry and exit, a review still to come, and interchange left open.

Hindustan Times reports the same six stations, the same hours, and the interchange staying open. NDTV reports the six-station closure and a Traffic Police diversion advisory. Hindu Business Line, with PTI, reports entry and exit shut at six stations ahead of a planned march. The Guardian reports stations shut ahead of a planned march. The check found no conflict on the six-station list. The framing differs: Hindustan Times stresses the day after what the court said on Sunday, and NDTV frames the closure ahead of the INDIA bloc march. Both of those frames are in the reports. This note keeps both, and it keeps them as frames.

A day earlier, on 11 October, the Supreme Court said the metro and the railways should function normally unless a public-order restriction under Article 19(2) is needed. Hindustan Times is the report this check uses for that Sunday line. This note uses the word "said". It places Sunday's line next to what DMRC announced for Monday. The pairing is the contrast: what the court said, and what the operator announced the next day for security reasons, on a clock that ran until 4 pm. This note does not add a legal finding to that pairing.

The closure was announced ahead of the INDIA bloc's planned march. NDTV is the report for the Traffic Police diversion advisory. This note stops at the advisory. It does not say what became of the march. At the time of the brief, a march result was not in hand, and this note does not supply one.

Saturday is context only. The brief records that Saturday's closure covered 45 stations, for the CJP protest. Monday's list is narrower: the six stations named above, for the hours DMRC set. This note does not recap detentions from Saturday. It uses Saturday only as the wider closure that came earlier in the same week.
