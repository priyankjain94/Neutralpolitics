This note is verified. The series is NP EXPLAINS. The carousel was posted at 4:52 pm IST on 28 October 2026. The stamp is VERIFIED. The cover is an NP graphic. The graphics are NP-made. No photograph is used. The package names PIB Cabinet decisions as a source. The PIB release was not fetched on its own in this build. The linked reports are Business Standard, The Times of India and India Today.

On 28 October 2026 the Union Cabinet approved an increase of 3 percentage points in dearness allowance and dearness relief. Dearness allowance is the employees' payment. Dearness relief is the pensioners' payment. Both will be 63 percent of basic pay, or of basic pension. They were 60 percent. The new rate is effective from 1 July 2026. Arrears run from July.

The rate is not a raise of the whole salary. It is the dearness rate, and it applies to basic pay, or to basic pension. A reader who treats the 3 points as 3 percent of the full pay is reading past what the Cabinet decided.

India Today gives an example. On a basic pay of ₹18,000, the allowance moves from ₹10,800 a month to ₹11,340 a month. The difference is ₹540 a month. Those three numbers are India Today's example. This note does not extend the example to any other basic pay.

Union Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw says about 1.16 crore people benefit. India Today puts the split at about 50 lakh employees and about 68 lakh pensioners. Those are two accounts of the same decision. This note does not add them into a third total. The annual cost to the government is ₹10,016 crore. Business Standard and India Today are the reports named for that cost.

The previous change was announced in April. It took the rate from 58 percent to 60 percent, with effect from 1 January 2026.

Dearness allowance is paid against the cost of living. It is revised twice a year, in January and in July. Dearness relief is the same kind of payment for pensioners, on basic pension. The figures on this page were rechecked at about 4:55 pm IST on 28 October, the package says, against Business Standard, The Times of India and India Today.

Read together, the lines that stay inside the decision are these. The date is 28 October 2026. The change is 3 percentage points. The rate moves from 60 percent to 63 percent of basic pay or basic pension. It runs from 1 July 2026, with arrears from July. India Today's example, on a basic pay of ₹18,000, is ₹540 more a month. About 1.16 crore people benefit, Vaishnaw says, and India Today splits that as about 50 lakh employees and about 68 lakh pensioners. The annual cost is ₹10,016 crore. The April change was 58 percent to 60 percent from 1 January 2026.
