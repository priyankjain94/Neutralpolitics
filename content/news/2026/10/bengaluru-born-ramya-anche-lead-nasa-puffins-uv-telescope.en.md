This note is NP EXPLAINS. The stamp is verified. University of Arizona News, dated 6 October 2026, and The Hindu, dated 16 October 2026 at 6:00 am IST, were read in full at about 4:56 pm IST on 17 October. The NASA Science Astrophysics Pioneers page, last updated 21 September 2026, returned a page at about 5:09 pm IST. The reel was posted at 7:16 pm IST on 17 October. The caption's hashtag is NPExplains. There is no photograph of Dr Anche in this post.

NASA has given a University of Arizona team $20 million for PUFFINS. University of Arizona News says NASA awarded $20 million. The Hindu reports the same sum. The NASA Pioneers page, in the package, describes a $20 million cost cap for Pioneers. The name expands to Polarimetry in the Ultraviolet to Find Features in INterStellar dust. It is a small satellite, a SmallSat, under the Astrophysics Pioneers programme. The principal investigator is Dr Ramya Anche, an assistant research professor at Steward Observatory. University of Arizona says the mission is in an early design phase.

What the telescope is to do, in those reports, is study the light of about 70 stars with a 10-inch ultraviolet telescope, so that scientists can understand how the small dust grains between stars form and behave. The NASA page confirms PUFFINS, the principal investigator, and ultraviolet light from about 70 stars.

The launch year is not the same in every source, and this note does not choose. University of Arizona, the lead institution, in its 6 October release, says the launch target is 2030. The Hindu, on 16 October, says the same target. The NASA Pioneers page, updated on 21 September, says "After its launch in 2029." The caption uses the 2030 target from the university and The Hindu, and notes that the NASA page says 2029. Both years stay on the page. The mission has not launched.

The India connection is in The Hindu, not in the University of Arizona release. The Hindu says Dr Anche is Bengaluru-born and grew up in Devanahalli, near Bengaluru, and that she joined the Indian Institute of Astrophysics in 2012. It says the team is roughly 40 people, and that two Indian institutes are on it: the Inter-University Centre for Astronomy and Astrophysics in Pune, and the Indian Institute of Astrophysics in Bengaluru. University of Arizona describes a joint effort with McDonald Observatory at the University of Texas at Austin and an international network. That release does not name IUCAA, IIA, or Bengaluru. The Hindu also says the Astrophysics Pioneers programme is especially for early-career scientists who are leading a NASA mission for the first time. That sentence is The Hindu's description of the programme.

The reel is not an ISRO mission. The caption says so, and the sources used here are NASA, the University of Arizona, and The Hindu.

The moving picture is not PUFFINS. It is a scientific visualisation of the Horsehead Nebula made from Hubble data. The on-screen credit is NASA, ESA and STScI / Hubble. The asset page credits the video to Gregory Bacon, Tiffany Borders-Davis, Lisa Frattare and Frank Summers at STScI, and says the central Hubble image is augmented by ground-based data from the European Southern Observatory. The label on the reel is that this is a visualisation of interstellar dust from Hubble data, not the new telescope. No NASA logos are used. The reel's music is from Pixabay, and the package says that track needs no credit.

What is agreed is the award, the investigator, the telescope's job, and that design is early. What differs is 2029 against 2030. What the picture is not is PUFFINS.
