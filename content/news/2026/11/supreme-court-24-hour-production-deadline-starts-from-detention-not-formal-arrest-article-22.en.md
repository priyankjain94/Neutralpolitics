This note is verified. The series is NP EXPLAINS. The stamp is VERIFIED. The carousel was posted at 1:29 pm IST on 5 November 2026. The cover is a Neutral Politics graphic. It is typographic. No photograph is used. No court video is used.

On 5 November 2026 the Supreme Court held that the constitutional deadline of 24 hours, under Article 22(2), for producing a person before a magistrate runs from the moment of detention. Detention, in this holding, is the moment liberty is curtailed. The clock does not wait for the paperwork of a formal arrest. The Hindu, LiveLaw and PTI report that holding. The bench is Justices Ujjal Bhuyan and Atul S. Chandurkar.

The accounts of the judgment say the bench held that an artificial distinction between detention and arrest is constitutionally unsustainable under Article 22(2) and Section 56 of the BNSS. That sentence is the reports' account of the bench. This page does not set it in quotation marks. The judgment text itself is not reproduced here.

What may be left out of the 24 hours is narrow. The Hindu reports one exception: the time needed for the journey from the place of arrest to the magistrate. Inquiry time cannot be excluded. Investigation time before the formal arrest cannot be excluded. Those limits are in the reports of the ruling. A magistrate must record the exact date and time of detention. That duty is also in the reports.

The case the Court decided is reported by LiveLaw. Four people accused in an NDPS case were detained at about 2 pm on 29 September 2025. Formal arrest was shown at about 11 am on 30 September 2025. The same reports describe the detention before that formal arrest as about 18 hours. This page keeps the two clock times and the "about 18 hours" line together, as they are reported. It does not substitute a count of its own. The Court ordered the immediate release of the four for the breach of Article 22(2). The order, as reported, is for that breach. This page does not say the NDPS case was closed, and it does not say the four were cleared of the accusation.

LiveLaw also reports what the Court did with earlier High Court views. Views that excluded the time before formal arrest were held incorrect. The views named are the Orissa view in Aryaman Pattanaik, the Kerala view in Arun K Thomas, and the Madras view in Kannan. The Kerala view in Biswajith Mandal, which counted from detention, was approved. Those names and courts are LiveLaw's, as the package records them. This page does not add citations those reports do not give.

The Hindu's account says the Court held that fundamental rights take precedence over the bail restrictions in special laws when a production timeline is breached. The special laws named in that context are UAPA, PMLA and NDPS. The point, as the reports draw it, is the production timeline. It is the precedence of fundamental rights when that timeline is breached.

## What the ruling does not do

Formal arrest is not abolished. The ruling moves the start of the 24-hour clock. It does not end the act of formal arrest.

This is not a blanket release of undertrials. The immediate release reported here is for the four NDPS accused, because Article 22(2) was breached in their case.

The bail bars under UAPA, PMLA and NDPS are not struck down. When the deadline for production before a magistrate is breached, fundamental rights take precedence over those restrictions. That is the reported holding. The restrictions themselves remain on the books, as the reports describe the ruling.
