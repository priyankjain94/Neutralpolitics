## Agniveer extension

The Union Cabinet on Wednesday approved a change to the Agnipath scheme. Chief of Defence Staff General N. S. Raja Subramani announced it at the Cabinet briefing at the National Media Centre in New Delhi. Twenty-five percent of Agniveers will still be enrolled as regulars on merit after four years. The remaining 75 percent will be offered two more years, with higher pay. After six years, those who are willing can be considered for regular enrolment under objective selection criteria. This is not a rollback of the scheme, and it is not a permanent job for every Agniveer.

The first batch joined in 2022 and was due to complete four years in December 2026. The Economic Times reports that about 18,000 Agniveers of that batch were due to leave in December, and that a written exam will come before the physical test. The Hindu reports that those not retained after six years will not get a government-job reservation on that basis. Defence Minister Rajnath Singh called the change part of continuing structural reforms. That is his description. ## Two-child norm

On Wednesday a bench of Justices P. S. Narasimha and Alok Aradhe said laws that penalise having more than two children have lost their relevance for local-body elections. The court noted that India's total fertility rate has fallen from 3 in 2003 to 1.9, below the replacement level of 2.1, and that the fear of overpopulation has crumbled. Those words are the court's, as LiveLaw, The Indian Express and Bar & Bench report them. The bench referred the 2003 ruling in Javed v State of Haryana, which had upheld the norm, to a larger bench. The norm is not struck down. Its validity is not decided.

The case before the bench is that of former sarpanch Mangala Bhimrao Ingle of Kakoda gram panchayat in Maharashtra, disqualified over a third child under section 14(1)(j-1) of the Maharashtra Village Panchayats Act. The Bombay High Court had upheld the disqualification in August 2025. The Supreme Court stayed the proceedings. This page does not say the sarpanch has been reinstated. The court said such laws affect reproductive autonomy under Article 21 and keep a class of citizens out of representation. Amicus Rukmini Bobde's compilation, as reported, lists seven states that repealed the norm and 15 that never had it, and names Assam, Goa, Gujarat, Maharashtra, Odisha and Uttarakhand among those that still do. ## Andhra civic polls

Late on Tuesday, the TDP-led alliance with Jana Sena and the BJP was ahead in Andhra Pradesh's urban local body elections, on counts that are not a final State Election Commission tally. The caption names mayor or chair posts in Greater Visakhapatnam, Guntur, Tirupati and Kurnool for the alliance, and the Anantapur mayor post for the YSRCP. Reports name Mukka Sravani in Greater Visakhapatnam, K. Lakshmi Aparna in Guntur, M. Srinivas Prasad in Tirupati, Hemalatha in Chittoor, Abida in Machilipatnam, Pratyusha in Kurnool, and Jana Sena's Reddy Appalanaidu in Eluru. The YSRCP's Ramesh Goud was elected mayor of Anantapur, where reports say TDP rebels split the vote and the BJP lost the seat it contested.

IANS late-night tallies say the alliance won 73 chairperson posts and led in 8, while the YSRCP won 1 and led in 2. Those are not an SEC consolidated final. Seventeen chairperson posts were decided unopposed, all for the alliance. The Times of India puts the alliance at about 95 percent of ward seats in its account. Counting was on 3 November at 138 centres for 92 bodies. Turnout was 62.16 percent, the State Election Commission via IANS. Counting in Chirala was moved to 6 November because of rain from Cyclone Murasu. TDP's Nara Lokesh called the result a mandate to serve, not to celebrate. That is his statement. YSRCP chief Jagan Mohan Reddy alleged rigging, and alleged complicity by the commission and the police. That claim is alleged. It is his, and it is not stated here as fact. ## Kedartal

PTI reports that rescue teams recovered two more bodies on Wednesday near Kedartal in Uttarkashi, taking the toll from the 29 October avalanche to four. Four people are still missing. The [3 November roundup](/news/2026/11/india-world-in-24-hours-3-nov-2026) had the toll at two bodies and six missing. No one is named on this page. SDRF and Nehru Institute of Mountaineering teams are digging by hand through 10 to 12 feet of snow. Uttarkashi district magistrate Prashant Arya is overseeing the search, the reports say. The group had eight people: five trekkers from Bankura in West Bengal, two from Uttar Pradesh, and a local guide. This page does not say the four who are missing are dead. ## Auckland ODI

India beat New Zealand by five wickets under the DLS method in the first one-day international in Auckland on Wednesday, and lead the five-match series 1-0. New Zealand were all out for 185 in 40.2 overs. Arshdeep Singh took 4 for 70, Gurnoor Brar 3 for 44, and Kamboj 2 for 22. The rain-revised target was 143 in 32 overs. India reached it in 22.4 overs at 143 for 5. Virat Kohli scored 74 off 51 balls. Shubman Gill scored 37. Reports say Kohli now has the most runs in ODI chases, passing Sachin Tendulkar, 8,741 to 8,720. This page does not use a claim of a first Indian win in Auckland in 23 years. ## US midterms

The Associated Press has called the Senate and the House of Representatives for the Democrats, which the posted roundup reads as control of both chambers after Tuesday's midterm elections. Final seat totals are not given. Counting is still open on this page. The AP says Democrats won at least seven Senate seats, in Kansas, Iowa, Texas, Ohio, Maine, North Carolina and South Carolina. That "at least seven" is the AP's line, not a finished tally of either chamber. Reuters reports that Adam Hamilton is the first Democrat to win a Senate seat in Kansas since the Great Depression, and that Texas governor Greg Abbott lost to Democrat Gina Hinojosa. The posted caption, citing the AP and NBC, says Abdul El-Sayed of Michigan is the first Muslim elected to the US Senate. The new Congress sits on 3 January 2027. The slide is a 1997 file of the Capitol, Architect of the Capitol / Wikimedia Commons, public domain, not this election night.

## Ohio governor

Democrat Amy Acton defeated Indian-American Republican Vivek Ramaswamy for governor of Ohio. The Associated Press, NBC, CBS and CNN called the race. The posted caption says Acton is Ohio's first woman governor-elect. Reports also say she is the first Democrat to win the office since 2006. ## Iran

President Donald Trump confirmed that US forces struck a newly built Iranian anti-aircraft facility on Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz. In a Fox News interview on 3 November, carried by AFP, he said Iran had built the facility and that the United States hit it as soon as it was completed. He called it a minor attack. Those are his words. US officials had earlier described limited defensive strikes. That earlier account is in [Blasts reported on Iran's Qeshm Island](/news/2026/11/explosions-iran-qeshm-island-us-officials-defensive-strikes-hormuz). The Israeli military told AFP it was not involved. Iran's deputy foreign minister, Gharibabadi, said Tehran retains the right to legitimate self-defence. President Pezeshkian said no one will benefit from this war and that Iran still wants dialogue. Those are their statements. Qatar's foreign ministry said both sides are reviewing proposals and that it is hopeful of an interim deal. No deal is announced here. This page gives no casualty figure and no damage figure, and it does not say a war has resumed. The package notes that the strait is a key route for India's crude oil and LPG imports.
