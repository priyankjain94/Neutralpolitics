This note is verified. The series is NP EXPLAINS. The carousel was posted at 10:52 am IST on 2 November 2026. The stamp is VERIFIED. The cover is a Neutral Politics graphic: a typographic card and a simple drawn outline of Nepal with Dolpa marked. No photograph is used. A picture carried with the DT Next report is not used here. The six tourists are not named.

Six Indian tourists, two women and four men, had been stranded by heavy snowfall in Nepal's Dolpa district. The place in the reports is the Shey-Phoksundo National Park area, near the Numla/Wagla pass, after a visit to Shey-Phoksundo lake. Contact with the group had been lost since 1 November.

A Nepal Army helicopter could not land on Sunday 1 November because of bad weather. That flight did not complete a rescue. On Monday morning, 2 November, a private helicopter took all six out. Dolpa police spokesperson Inspector Binaya Singh is the officer named for that rescue. This page does not say the Army rescued them.

They are being treated at the government hospital in Dune. Their condition is out of danger. Chief District Officer Junu Dhakal confirmed that all are safe. The package also records that officer's name as Junu (Hamal) Dhakal.

The district administration says the group went to Upper Dolpa without the required permits, despite warnings about snowfall. That sentence is the officials' statement. This note does not add a judgement of its own.

An earlier roundup on this site recorded the stranding and said safety had not been confirmed then. [India & World, 1 Nov: 8 stories](/news/2026/11/india-world-in-24-hours-1-nov-2026). This page is the later report of the rescue. It does not replace that roundup.

Read together, the reported lines are these. Six Indian tourists, two women and four men, were out of contact from 1 November near the Numla/Wagla pass in the Shey-Phoksundo National Park area of Dolpa, after visiting the lake. An Army helicopter could not land that day because of the weather. A private helicopter rescued them on the morning of 2 November, Inspector Binaya Singh says. They are at the government hospital in Dune, out of danger, and Chief District Officer Junu Dhakal says all are safe. The district administration says they went to Upper Dolpa without the required permits, despite snowfall warnings.
