This note is verified. The series is NP EXPLAINS. The carousel was posted at 12:49 pm IST on 1 November 2026. The stamp is VERIFIED. The release itself is dated Saturday 31 October 2026. The source is the Ministry of Finance press release, Press Information Bureau PRID 2330997. A recheck at about 12:50 pm IST on 1 November found that the total and all 28 state figures match, and that they sum to ₹1,09,019 crore. The six cards are NP-made typographic cards. No photograph is used. The state table on the cards is drawn from the PIB figures.

On 31 October the Union Finance Ministry released an additional instalment of tax devolution of ₹1,09,019 crore to the state governments. The caption also rounds that sum as ₹1.09 lakh crore. The instalment is over and above the regular monthly instalment. It is the second additional instalment this financial year. An additional instalment of the same amount was released on 1 August.

This is not a new scheme. The caption says it is the states' own share, given in advance. States receive 41 percent of the divisible pool of central taxes. That is the constitutional share. The Hindu and Mint are the reports named for the point that this share is normally paid in 14 instalments a year. This page does not call the release a bonus. It does not call it a relief package.

The ministry says the release is in view of the festive season, so that states can accelerate capital spending and finance development and welfare expenditure. That purpose is the ministry's statement. Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman said on X that the release is supported by stronger economic activity and buoyant tax collections. Those words are hers. Mint is the report that carries the post.

Each state's amount follows the Finance Commission formula. The caption says the criteria include population, area and income. It says "such as", so this note does not treat those three as a closed list. A larger number in the table is the formula's result for that state. It is not a sign that the Centre preferred that state. A smaller number is not neglect.

Read together, the release is this. The day of the release is 31 October 2026. The sum is ₹1,09,019 crore, in addition to the regular monthly devolution, and it is the second additional instalment this financial year after the same amount on 1 August. It is 41 percent of the divisible pool, normally paid in 14 instalments, paid here in advance. It is not a new scheme. The ministry ties it to the festive season, capital spending, and development and welfare expenditure. The Finance Commission formula sets each state's line, including criteria such as population, area and income. The 28 figures above are the PIB table and sum to the total. Nirmala Sitharaman's line on X, as Mint reports it, is that the release is supported by stronger economic activity and buoyant tax collections.
