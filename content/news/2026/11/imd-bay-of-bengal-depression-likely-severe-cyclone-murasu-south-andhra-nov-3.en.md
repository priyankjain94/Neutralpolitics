## Update, 3 November

This update was posted at 8:55 am IST on 3 November 2026. The latest line is IMD's coastal bulletin at 08:36 IST. Severe Cyclonic Storm Murasu has crossed the south Andhra Pradesh coast between Nellore and Narasapur, close to Ongole, near 15.53°N, 80.22°E, between 3:30 am and 5:30 am IST. Some reports spell the northern point Narsapur. The wind speed at the crossing was 100 to 110 km/h, gusting to 120 km/h. Hindustan Times and The Hindu, citing the state disaster authority and IMD, say the landfall process began around 1 am near Kothapatnam in Prakasam district.

At 08:36 IST the centre was over south coastal Andhra, about 40 km southwest of Bapatla. IMD expects the system to weaken to a cyclonic storm by noon and a deep depression by night. The Times of India reports those stages as 80 to 90 km/h by noon and 55 to 65 km/h by midnight. A storm-surge warning of 1 to 1.5 metres remains for low-lying areas, and the caption says it is expected to ease. The Times of India and IMD put that warning on low-lying parts of Bapatla, Krishna and Prakasam. Kothapatnam recorded 16.4 cm of rain from 08:30 IST on 2 November to 05:00 IST on 3 November.

On the ground, the Andhra Pradesh disaster authority, Hindustan Times, The Hindu and The Times of India report uprooted trees and water in low-lying areas of Prakasam, Bapatla and Nellore. The Times of India also reports waterlogged roads in Vijayawada. Three NDRF teams, 90 personnel, are deployed. Tamil Nadu has sent 500 power staff in 25 teams. No official death toll had been reported as of about 08:45 am IST. Reports differ on power cuts: The Hindu says no disruption was reported, and The Times of India reports outages in parts. This note does not say the power was cut across the state, and it does not use a 150 km/h gust figure.

Until an all-clear, stay indoors. Stay away from fallen trees and power lines. Follow IMD and official updates, not rumours. Emergency 112. Helpline 1070. This note does not call the storm very severe.

## Update, 2 November

This update was posted at 8:44 am IST on 2 November 2026. The latest line is IMD National Bulletin No. 11, bulletin BOB/09/2026, issued at 05:00 IST on 2 November, for the position at 02:30 IST. A check of mausam.imd.gov.in at 08:41 IST found Bulletin No. 12 not yet published, so this section stays with No. 11. The paragraphs under "Earlier, 1 November" are the earlier note. They are not the latest line.

At 02:30 IST, Cyclone Murasu was a severe cyclonic storm, about 260 km east-northeast of Chennai, moving northwest at 20 km/h. IMD's forecast, which is not a report that landfall has happened, is a crossing of the south Andhra Pradesh coast between Nellore and Narsapur, near Ongole, between 11:30 pm on 2 November and 4 am on 3 November. The forecast winds are 100 to 110 km/h, gusting to 120 km/h. This note does not call the storm very severe. It gives no death toll.

IMD's forecast also includes a storm surge of 1 to 1.5 metres in low-lying parts of south coastal Andhra Pradesh, in Bapatla, Krishna and Prakasam. Isolated extremely heavy rain is forecast for coastal Andhra Pradesh. Heavy rain alerts cover Rayalaseema and Tamil Nadu.

Schools and colleges in Chennai district and Tiruvallur district are closed on 2 November. Those closures are the official notices of the District Collectors.

If the administration in coastal Andhra Pradesh says so, people in kutcha houses and low-lying areas should move. During the crossing, stay indoors. Fishermen should not go to sea. Follow IMD and your district collector. Do not follow rumours.

## Earlier, 1 November

This note is verified. The series is NP EXPLAINS. The carousel was posted at 8:58 am IST on 1 November 2026. The stamp is VERIFIED. The figures below are from the India Meteorological Department's sea area bulletin issued at 06:00 IST on 1 November, and from the Met Centre Amaravati sub-division warnings issued the same day. A recheck at about 9:00 am IST found those News Puller numbers matched. Forecasts can change. Follow IMD at mausam.imd.gov.in and on the MAUSAM app, and follow the Andhra Pradesh government's official advisories. Fishermen should follow IMD's sea warning. This note is not a rumour line.

This is a new weather system. It is not Cyclone Arnab. Arnab crossed the Andhra Pradesh and Yanam coasts on 28 October. That earlier note is [Arnab crosses near Narsapur, weakens](/news/2026/10/imd-depression-bay-of-bengal-very-severe-cyclone-andhra-oct-28). This page does not merge the two.

At 11:30 pm IST on 31 October, the depression was over the southeast Bay of Bengal, near 10.9°N and 87.5°E. It was about 830 km east-southeast of Chennai, about 880 km south-southeast of Visakhapatnam, and about 900 km southeast of Machilipatnam. It was moving west-northwest at 22 km/h.

IMD's forecast is this. A deep depression by noon on 1 November. A cyclonic storm by midnight on 1 November. A severe cyclonic storm on 2 November. It is likely to cross the south Andhra Pradesh coast between Nellore and Narsapur by the morning of 3 November, with winds of 100 to 110 km/h, gusting to 120 km/h. By the morning is the timing in the bulletin. This note does not add an hour inside that morning.

An earlier IMD outlook was a cyclonic storm near the Andhra Pradesh coast on the evening of 2 November, with winds of 80 to 90 km/h. The latest bulletins upgrade that outlook to a severe cyclonic storm.

If it becomes a cyclone, the name will be Murasu. Murasu is Tamil for drum, and it is India's contribution to the list. The name is not official yet. This page says the depression is likely to become Cyclone Murasu. It does not say that IMD has already given the name.

For coastal Andhra Pradesh, the IMD warnings issued on 1 November are these. Heavy rain on 1 November. Extremely heavy rain on 2 November. Very heavy rain on 3 November. Heavy rain on 4 November. For Rayalaseema, very heavy rain is warned for 2 and 3 November. This note does not give an evacuation count. It does not give a damage figure.
