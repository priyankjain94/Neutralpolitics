The Supreme Court on 28 September floated one way to recover unpaid traffic e-challans. A bench of Justices JB Pardiwala and KV Viswanathan suggested that states and Union territories consider linking those dues to electricity bills. The suggestion came while the court discussed how unpaid penalties might be recovered. It was not an order directing any state to change billing. It does not put challans on power bills, and it is not a binding nationwide rule already in force.

Bar & Bench and PTI reported the discussion in court. Hindustan Times and India Today carried supporting accounts of the same hearing. The note drawn from those reports states the limit in plain words: the Supreme Court floated the idea, but it is not an order, and a power bill does not change today. A later note uses the same words. The facts of the hearing are unchanged.

The sums that travel with the hearing are the ones the court was told, and the ones the reports cite. The court was told that about ₹45,000 crore is due and about ₹25,000 crore has been recovered, leaving around ₹20,000 crore. The reports put the same figures on traffic challans: roughly ₹45,000 crore due, about ₹25,000 crore recovered, and around ₹20,000 crore still outstanding. Those are reported figures placed before the court. They are not described as a total the court itself calculated or certified.

Linking a challan to an electricity bill was one option in that talk, not the whole of it. The bench also discussed blacklisting vehicles on Parivahan, blocking ownership transfers, and random checks. Each of those sits in the same discussion of recovery. The reports do not describe any of them as a direction that has already taken effect across the country. A reader can treat them as ideas the bench raised. The reports do not treat them as steps that states must now carry out.

A suggestion that states and Union territories consider a link is not a rule that they must use one. The court did not direct a named state, or every state, to add a traffic penalty to the next power bill. It did not tell an electricity company to collect a fine. The practical line in the reports is short: the bill does not change today. Unpaid challans have not been added to power bills nationwide on the strength of this hearing.

If the suggestion is ever used, that would be a later step. Any implementation would require action by the relevant authorities, and that action should be separately confirmed. Until a state, a Union territory, or another authority is reported to have taken such a step, the hearing remains a discussion of a recovery option. The discussion, by itself, does not change a bill.

The reports do not give a next date of hearing. They do not set out a written order that adopts the electricity-bill link. They do not name who placed the ₹45,000 crore and ₹25,000 crore figures before the court. They say the court was told those figures, and they cite them. What they do fix is the shape of the hearing: two judges, a suggestion aimed at states and Union territories, other recovery ideas, and a line between that talk and an order.

## What's next

Nothing in this hearing changes an electricity bill. The court invited states and Union territories to consider linking unpaid e-challans to power dues, alongside blacklisting on Parivahan, blocked ownership transfers, and random checks. If an authority acts on any of those ideas, the step is separate and would need its own confirmation. Until then, unpaid traffic challans are not on power bills because of this hearing.
