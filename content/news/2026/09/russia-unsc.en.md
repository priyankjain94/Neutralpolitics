Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov on Saturday said Russia supports India's candidature for a permanent seat on the United Nations Security Council. India Today reported the remarks from his address to the General Assembly. He also backed Brazil, and he opposed extra permanent seats for Western countries. The speech is support for those candidatures. India Today also set out how the UN Charter is amended. A statement by one permanent member sits inside that rule. It does not itself add a seat.

India Today quoted him: "UN Security Council reform cannot involve any additional seats being allocated to Western countries, especially to Germany and Japan, who are now on the path to militarism and revanchism." He said: "We are in favour of broadening the representation of Asia, Africa, and Latin America. We support Brazil's and India's candidatures for a permanent seat." He said Africa's interests must be addressed. The words about Germany and Japan are his. He also called for a larger place for Asia, Africa and Latin America.

TASS reported his press conference after that speech, at the general debate of the 81st session, on 26 September. He said the West is already overrepresented and that Moscow cannot allow new "Western seats." TASS quoted him: "We cannot provide for or allow any new 'Western seats,' because the West is already overrepresented." He again excluded extra seats "especially" for Germany and Japan, "which have embarked on a path of militarization and revanchism." TASS said he repeated Russia's support for permanent seats for Brazil and India, while meeting Africa's interests in line with the Ezulwini Consensus and the Sirte Declaration. TASS names those two documents. It does not quote their text.

Other speakers in the same debate, as India Today reported them, also addressed India's bid. Mauritian Foreign Minister Dhananjay Ramful said Mauritius supports the G4 and L69 positions on Council reform, and supports India's candidature for a permanent seat, "commensurate with its constructive role in global affairs." Portugal's Prime Minister Luis Montenegro called for a more representative Council, with permanent representation for Africa, Brazil and India. Those are further statements of support. India Today did not report them as a vote.

A different view on who should sit permanently was also on the record. Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi called for the Council to reflect current realities, with expansion in both permanent and non-permanent seats. President Hilda Heine of the Marshall Islands said: "The Marshall Islands affirms its longstanding support for Japan and its G4 partners to achieve permanent membership on the UN Security Council." Lavrov's objection to new Western seats, and his naming of Germany and Japan, is the other side of that argument. This article reports both as the speakers gave them. The reports use the labels G4 and L69. They do not list the members.

Sierra Leone's President Julius Maada Bio said the Council still reflects 1945, and that Africa, with more than 1.6 billion people, has no permanent seat. Ghana's President John Dramani Mahama said reform is required for the United Nations to stay credible. Neither report said a permanent African seat was agreed.

India Today reported the Council as it stands. Five permanent members, China, France, Russia, the United Kingdom and the United States, hold a veto. Ten non-permanent members serve two-year terms. An amendment to the Charter needs a two-thirds majority in the General Assembly and ratification by two-thirds of member states, including all five permanent members. Lavrov's support for India is the stated position of one of those five.

## What's next

Any change in who holds a permanent seat has to pass the amendment test India Today reported: a two-thirds vote in the General Assembly, then ratification by two-thirds of member states, including every permanent member. These reports give no date for such a vote. They record the speeches in the 81st session's general debate.
