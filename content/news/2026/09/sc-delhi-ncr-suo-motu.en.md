The Supreme Court on Monday, 28 September 2026, took suo motu cognisance of recent sexual assaults on minors in Delhi and the National Capital Region. A bench of Justices J.B. Pardiwala and K. Vinod Chandran directed that a suo motu public-interest petition be registered and listed, Bar and Bench reported.

The bench referred to recent cases, including one involving a 17-year-old at Aastha Kunj park near Kalkaji, one at Swaroop Nagar, and an assault on a bus travelling from Greater Noida toward Delhi. This report does not name any survivor and does not describe the assaults.

The judges said they could not help but draw a "painful parallel" with the 2012 Nirbhaya case, and asked whether lessons had been learnt. They said public spaces — parks, roads, buses, the Metro and subways — must not become high-risk zones because of poor patrolling, surveillance or illumination. Statements of concern without action, the court said, are not enough. It sought a measurable response and accountability from the authorities, including Delhi Police. Hindustan Times reported that a response was sought from Delhi Police. The bench also framed Article 21 as the right to live with dignity, free from violence.

The order at this stage is to register and list the case and to seek a response. It is not a finding on any individual investigation.
