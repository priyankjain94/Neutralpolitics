The Delhi High Court on Wednesday, 30 September 2026, dismissed the bail appeals of Umar Khalid and Sharjeel Imam. The bench was Justices Prathiba M. Singh and Dinesh Bhatt. Bar and Bench, LiveLaw, India Today and Hindustan Times all name those two judges and all report the dismissal. This is a ruling on bail. It is not a verdict on the charges. Neither man is described here as guilty. Both are accused.

The case, as LiveLaw, India Today and Hindustan Times describe it, is the alleged larger conspiracy behind the February 2020 riots in north-east Delhi, under the Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act. Bar and Bench calls it the Delhi riots conspiracy case. "Alleged" is the word those reports use for the conspiracy, and it is the word this report keeps. Being an accused in that case is not a finding that the allegation is proved. The High Court, in the accounts checked, did not try the charges. It decided appeals against a refusal of bail.

Those appeals challenged the trial court's refusal of bail on 4 July 2026. LiveLaw, Hindustan Times and Bar and Bench date that refusal to 4 July. India Today also uses 4 July, and calls it a third rejection. The date is shared. A count of earlier refusals is not.

The limit on a fresh bail plea is the Supreme Court's, from 5 January 2026. Khalid and Imam may renew bail only when protected witnesses have been examined, or when one year from that order has passed, whichever is earlier. LiveLaw, India Today and Hindustan Times all use "whichever is earlier". Hindustan Times says "expiry of one year". Bar and Bench paraphrases it as bail after a year or after the protected witnesses have been examined. This report uses the shared condition: protected witnesses, or one year from 5 January 2026, whichever is earlier.

The High Court said it cannot change that condition. LiveLaw and India Today quote the bench: "This court is bound by judicial discipline." Hindustan Times reports that the court would not be in a position to modify the condition. Bar and Bench reports that granting bail would amount to modifying the Supreme Court order. Those are four ways of stating the same limit. The exact words on the discipline line are the LiveLaw and India Today quotation. The other two are the papers' account of what follows from it.

Nothing in the four reports is a decision that the charges are true or false. Each report is about appeals on bail. That is a procedural fact. No sentence in the material used here says the court pronounced on guilt. This report does not add one.

What this account leaves out is the argument around the men rather than the order. India Today and Hindustan Times carry police allegations that call them masterminds. LiveLaw quotes the Supreme Court calling them ideological drivers of the entire alleged conspiracy. Those are characterisations. They are not findings of this bail order, and they are not repeated as facts. India Today's "third" count and Bar and Bench's count, a third High Court denial for Khalid and a second for Imam, do not match, so neither is stated. A death and injury toll, and arrest dates, appear in India Today alone and are not used. Counsel names, the NIA Act section of the appeal, and arguments about a change in circumstances are not used. Bar and Bench reports a submission that bail can be sought only after January 2027. That is the submission. The order's wording, in the other three reports, remains one year from 5 January 2026 or the end of the protected-witness examination, whichever is earlier. A line that the High Court could not find fault with the trial court, and a line that five co-accused got bail on 5 January 2026, were left out.

## What's next

Khalid and Imam remain accused. The bail appeals are dismissed. On the Supreme Court's 5 January 2026 condition, as the High Court said it cannot modify it, a fresh bail plea waits on the protected witnesses or on one year from that order, whichever comes first. The charges are not decided by this ruling.
