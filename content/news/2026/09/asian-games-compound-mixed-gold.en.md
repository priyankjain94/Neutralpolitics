Chikitha Taniparthi and Sahil Jadhav won the compound mixed team gold at the Asian Games in Aichi-Nagoya on 30 September 2026. They beat South Korea 158–157. South Korea's pair, in the same accounts, was Park Jungyoon and Choi Yonghee. The final was level at 157–157 after four ends. India then won the shoot-off.

The shoot-off figure to use is the one on the official results page. That page, checked for this report, shows the final as India 158, South Korea 157, with regulation 157–157 and a shoot-off of 20–19. Business Standard records the same 20–19. The note with those sources is two arrows each: India 10 and 10, South Korea 10 and 9. Chosunbiz and an Indian Express live report are listed with that 20–19 reading. Firstpost writes the shoot-off as 10–9. The source note treats that as the final-arrow wording, not as a different match: Korea's 9 against India's 10. A shorter brief in the same material also says 10–9. This report states 20–19, because that is the official page and Business Standard, and it records 10–9 as Firstpost's way of describing the decider.

The semifinal was not a shoot-off. India beat Thailand 156–155. Outlook, Business Standard, the Indian Express live report, News18, and Hindustan Times and NDTV blogs reported that score. This is a different match from the women's compound team final earlier in the day, which has its own report.

It is Chikitha's second gold of the day. The first was the women's compound team title. Outlook, Business Standard and the Indian Express live report say so. This article does not retell that final. It also does not say this was Sahil Jadhav's first Asian Games gold. That line was one paper's and was not used.

Reports call the mixed gold India's eighth of these Games. Firstpost, News18 and the Indian Express live report use that count. It is their count. A Sportstar tally table at 07:45 IST still showed seven golds, and that was before this final. No refreshed table was seen after it. Eight is what those match reports say, not a number read off an updated medal table. An exact all-medal total, and India's rank after this gold, are not stated. The tables in the material do not agree.

Reports also say the win books India a quota place for Los Angeles 2028, where the compound mixed team event debuts. Firstpost, Outlook, Business Standard, the Indian Express live report and News18 carry that line. This report attributes it to them. It does not set out a rule, from one paper alone, that the Asian Games champion automatically earns the place.

What this account does not add is a record. India's 158 is not described as a world record, an Asian record or a Games record. The official page lists a Korea 160, dated 27 September, as those marks for this event. 158 is below that line. Arrow-by-arrow stories of who shot a 9 in which end of regulation were left out, because Business Standard and the Indian Express did not use the same wording. The men's compound team final was still pending and is not here.

## What's next

The agreed result is a 158–157 gold over South Korea, a 157–157 tie after four ends, a shoot-off recorded as 20–19 on the official page, and a 156–155 semifinal over Thailand. The eighth gold and the LA 2028 quota are what the reports say. The women's team gold remains a separate story.
