India's women's compound archery team beat China 238–234 in the Asian Games final on Wednesday, 30 September 2026, in Aichi-Nagoya. The team was Jyothi Surekha Vennam, Chikitha Taniparthi and Prithika Pradeep. The score that matters beside the gold is 238. Multiple outlets reported that 238 equalled the world record previously associated with South Korea. It did not break that mark.

The wording is consistent on that limit, and it is not the same as a new record. The Hindu's live account said India equalled the world record. Hindustan Times said 238 equals the world record. NDTV said India was matching the world record set by the South Koreans. The Times of India said India was matching South Korea's world record. Outlook India said India equalled a world record with South Korea. None of those lines says the mark was broken. This report follows them: equalled, or matched. Not broken.

The final score is the one the outlets share. India 238, China 234. The Hindu, Hindustan Times, the Indian Express, NDTV, the Times of India and Outlook India reported that result. The three names are in The Hindu, Hindustan Times, the Indian Express and NDTV. This account does not add an arrow-by-arrow description. End scores that one paper corrected, such as a single arrow changed from 10 to 9, were left out because each such detail sat with one outlet.

In the semifinal India beat South Korea 234–233. Hindustan Times, the Indian Express, NDTV, Outlook, the Times of India and The Hindu reported that win. The Hindu described it as a win by one point, which is the same margin as 234–233. South Korea is the country the world-record line is tied to, and South Korea is the semifinal opponent. Those are two facts. Beating South Korea by one point in the semifinal is not the same sentence as equalling the record in the final.

The gold is reported as India's seventh of these Games. The Indian Express live report called it the seventh gold. NDTV, the Times of India and Outlook India did so as well. The arithmetic in the notes is six golds before this match, plus this one. The Indian Express tally and Sportstar had India on six golds before the archery. When the tables were checked, they had not yet been refreshed to seven. The Indian Express table was still on six, as of 29 September. So seven is what the match reports say. It is not a number read off an updated medal table.

India was defending the title. The Indian Express called the team defending champions. NDTV said they were out to defend their compound women's team title. Both place the earlier win at Hangzhou in 2023. That is the title being retained. It is not a claim about every archery event at these Games.

What this report leaves out is as definite as the score. It does not give India's rank after the gold. One live line said the rank was rising to 9th. Another said top 10. No refreshed tally table was available, and the all-medal totals were still in conflict. It does not repeat a line that India also scored 238 against Mongolia in the round of 16. That figure was not in the agreed account, and it would blur the 238 that equalled the record in the final. It does not say this was the first archery gold of the edition. That was one live report. It does not say an official results page was opened to confirm the record. The record line rests on the outlets above.

## What's next

The result these reports agree on is a 238–234 gold over China, a 234–233 semifinal over South Korea, and a 238 that matched the world record associated with South Korea without breaking it. The seventh gold is the match reports' count, not a table that had already been updated.
