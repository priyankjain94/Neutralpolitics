The United Forum of Bank Unions has deferred a nationwide bank strike that was planned for 28–30 September. The decision is in UFBU Circular No. 2026/31, dated 27 September, and followed a meeting with the Indian Banks' Association at 9:30 pm on Sunday.

UFBU is an umbrella of seven unions: AIBEA, AIBOC, NCBE, AIBOA, BEFI, INBEF and INBOC. The circular was signed jointly by the constituent unions. Reports name C.H. Venkatachalam of AIBEA and Rupam Roy of AIBOC among those associated with the talks.

The unions' main demand is a five-day banking week, with all Saturdays off. Banks now close on the second and fourth Saturdays. The unions say this was agreed in principle in the 8 March 2024 settlement and sent to the government. Finance Ministry sources have said no such commitment was given. IBA agreed to form an IBA–UFBU high-level committee immediately to consider treating the remaining Saturdays as holidays. There is no commitment yet that the five-day week will be introduced. The two sides will also discuss changes to the performance-linked incentive scheme for officers in Scale IV and above, and residual issues from the March 2024 minutes.

The circular uses the word "deferred", not "withdrawn". Some headlines, including in The Hindu, said the strike was called off. No new strike date was announced. Banks were expected to function normally from Monday, 28 September. The strike would have coincided with the 30 September half-yearly closing.

Before the deferral, the Finance Ministry, with RBI approval, had asked all 12 public sector banks and regional rural banks to stay open on Sunday, 27 September. SBI, PNB, Canara Bank, Union Bank of India and Bank of Baroda were among them. AIBOA had asked members to skip that Sunday duty, so services could vary. UPI, net banking and ATMs were not part of the strike plan. HDFC Bank said it would run normally. Mint had earlier reported a one-day strike on 11 September and a threat of an indefinite strike from 26 October. That report does not say whether the October threat is also deferred.
