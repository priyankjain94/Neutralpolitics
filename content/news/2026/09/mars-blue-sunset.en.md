Sunsets on Mars do not look like sunsets on Earth. Near the Sun, the Martian sky can glow blue.

Fine dust in the Martian atmosphere lets blue light pass more easily and keeps that light close to the Sun. Red and yellow light scatter farther across the sky. The colours are effectively reversed from the familiar Earth pattern, where the sky is blue in daytime and the setting sun often looks red.

NASA's Curiosity rover captured its first colour image of a Martian sunset on 15 April 2015. The account used here cites NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory. No separate news-outlet URL was in the file.
