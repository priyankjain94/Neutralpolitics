The Supreme Court on Monday, 28 September 2026, directed the Central Board of Secondary Education to extend to students now in Class 6 the same transitional relief on a third language that is already available to Class 7. The bench was Chief Justice Surya Kant, Justice Joymalya Bagchi and Justice V. Mohana. Hindustan Times and LiveLaw reported the interim direction. The wider challenge to the three-language rollout, including how English is treated as a non-native language, remains pending. This is not a final ruling, and it is not a decision that the policy has been struck down.

The court spoke of giving Class 6 the same "comfort" as Class 7, and of a soft launch so that students are not disrupted in the middle of the year. Hindustan Times and India Today quoted the bench in terms that point to fuller implementation from April 2027.

Solicitor General Tushar Mehta told the court that about 99 per cent of more than 28,000 CBSE schools are compliant, and that about 1.2 per cent are in difficulty. He said the Centre was not inclined to exempt Class 6 this year. The court still ordered the same relief as for Class 7.

LiveLaw and The Times of India report that the Class 7-style relief means the third language is studied with internal assessment, and that there is no CBSE Class 10 board pass-or-fail consequence in that language, in an appearance-certificate framing. The Times of India also notes flexibility for students who already study two non-native languages: they may keep those and add one Bharatiya Bhasha. Other outlets stress the evaluation relief more than the language choice. This article records both as the Class 7-style package the court extended, and does not treat one outlet's fine print as the whole order.

Counter-affidavit timelines were set. The main petitions are still to be decided.
