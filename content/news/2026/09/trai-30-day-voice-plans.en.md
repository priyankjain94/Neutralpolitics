India's telecom regulator has required operators to sell voice-and-SMS-only plans on the same short validities they already use for data bundles. The Telecom Regulatory Authority of India notified the Telecom Consumers Protection (Thirteenth Amendment) Regulations, 2026, on 22 September 2026.

For every validity of 30 days or less that an operator sells as a bundled pack, it must also offer a voice-and-SMS-only special tariff voucher of that validity. It must offer at least one voice-and-SMS-only voucher with a longer validity. It must also offer a voice-and-SMS-only voucher that renews on the same calendar date each month. TRAI says the tariffs should carry an appropriate reduction compared with data packs. The rules do not fix a rupee price. Operators set the prices, and none had dropped when the account that accompanies this article was published.

The regulation followed a consultation that received 1,132 responses. TRAI said the aim is flexibility for low-income users and for people who want calls and messages without paying for data.

On a 28-day cycle, a year of recharges is 13, not 12. Rajya Sabha MP Raghav Chadha had raised that arithmetic, and the lack of voice-only plans, in Parliament on 11 March. He welcomed the notification. The welcome is his political response. The obligation on operators is the text TRAI notified on 22 September.

What changes for a subscriber is the menu, not a announced fare. A call-and-SMS pack of 30 days or less must exist beside the data pack of the same length, and it is supposed to cost less. Until an operator publishes a new price, the size of that cut is not known.
