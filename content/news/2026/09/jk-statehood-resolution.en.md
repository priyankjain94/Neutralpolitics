The Jammu and Kashmir Legislative Assembly on Monday, 28 September 2026, passed Chief Minister Omar Abdullah's resolution seeking the urgent restoration of full statehood to the Union Territory. The resolution had been moved on Friday, 25 September. The Indian Express and Deccan Herald reported that it passed amid a walkout by the BJP. Hindustan Times reported a majority vote after a long debate, and that the BJP left the House before the vote.

Hindustan Times reported that the passed text carries references to autonomy and special status. The Indian Express reported that 10 amendments were moved, and that eight of them sought to broaden the resolution to the special status the former state held before August 2019. This article does not say those amendments were all adopted, and it does not say Article 370 or Article 35A has been restored. The vote is the Assembly's demand to the Centre. It does not, by itself, return statehood.

Before the vote the sitting was stormy. BJP MLAs entered the well, tore business papers and raised slogans after Speaker Abdul Rahim Rather declined to step aside. The party had moved a motion under Rule 215(2), signed by 23 MLAs, to remove the Speaker. The Speaker said a motion of that kind is listed only after 14 days' notice.

The BJP's objection is to the framing. The party says it supports statehood, and objects to references to the June 2000 autonomy resolution and to the November 2024 resolution on special status and the pre-2019 position. Treasury benches and the National Conference say the text is only about restoring statehood.

An earlier account of the same sitting, filed while the debate was still on, said the resolution had not been passed. That was overtaken by the vote. The passage reported by The Indian Express, Deccan Herald and Hindustan Times is the later record.
