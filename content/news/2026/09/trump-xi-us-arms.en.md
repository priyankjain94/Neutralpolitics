US Ambassador to China David Perdue told Fox News on Sunday, 27 September, that President Donald Trump, while discussing arms sales, asked Chinese President Xi Jinping whether China "would like to buy some" American weapons. Perdue did not say when the question was put. The Hindu and The Times of India reported the same attributed remarks after Trump hosted Xi in Washington. The line is Perdue's account of what Trump asked. It is not a confirmed offer or a sale.

After Perdue spoke, the White House told the New York Times, as cited by The Guardian, that there are no plans to sell arms to China. The State Department said US law prohibits arms sales to China. On the material here, there is no recorded offer and no plan.

A Taiwan arms package of roughly $14 billion remains on hold after earlier talks. Trump has previously described sales to Taiwan as a negotiating chip, after a meeting in Beijing in May. Perdue said Trump is "as strong on Taiwan as I've ever seen", and that the United States is still required under the Taiwan Relations Act to help Taiwan defend itself.

Chinese state media, Xinhua, reported after the Washington visit that Xi pressed Trump on Taiwan and on opposing what Beijing calls Taiwan independence.

The balance of US policy on China and Taiwan sits in the Indo-Pacific, which is the region India also watches. This report does not say the Indian government took part in the exchange, and it does not infer a change in New Delhi's position from Perdue's interview.
