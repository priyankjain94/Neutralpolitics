The Army said one terrorist was killed in Operation Korag in Budgam on Tuesday, 29 September. That is the confirmed line, and it stops at what the Chinar Corps post said. The name, the alleged group, and the claim that the killing was a setback are officials' accounts. They are not in the Army statement that did not name him.

The Chinar Corps post on X, as quoted by The New Indian Express, the Times of India, Firstpost, the Indian Express and Greater Kashmir, said the Army, Jammu and Kashmir Police and CRPF carried out a joint search in the Korag area of Yousmarg, in Budgam, on specific intelligence. Troops challenged suspicious activity. Terrorists opened fire. One terrorist was neutralised. The post said the operation was in progress. Those are the sentences the outlets attribute to the Corps. This report does not add a route, a weapon, or a count of people beyond that one person.

The Army post did not name the person killed. Reports citing officials did. The New Indian Express said officials identified him as Sulaiman Shah, alias Musa. Firstpost reported the name Fauzi Musa, also known as Sulaiman Shah, and attributed that to Army officials. Republic World reported Sulaiman Shah, alias Fauzi Musa, and attributed that to security officials. Fauzi Musa appears in Firstpost and Republic World. It is not the name in The New Indian Express or, on this material, the Times of India. This report therefore uses Sulaiman Shah, alias Musa, as the officials' main identification, and Fauzi Musa as a name also reported. Both are alleged identifications. Neither is a name the Chinar Corps post supplied.

Officials go further, and those further lines stay labelled as theirs. The New Indian Express, Firstpost and Republic World say officials described him as a Lashkar-e-Taiba militant. That alleged link is not an independent finding here. The same outlets say officials linked him to an encounter in the Yousmarg hills on 5 September, which he allegedly escaped, and in which an associate named Yasir was killed. The New Indian Express and Firstpost, as officials' words, say he was wanted for attacks on both sides of the Pir Panjal. The New Indian Express quotes officials calling the killing the biggest setback for Lashkar-e-Taiba in Jammu and Kashmir. Republic World quotes officials calling it a major setback. "Biggest" and "major" are the officials' assessments. This report does not adopt them.

Some details in the same papers are left out because they are not the Army's place-name or because the papers do not agree. The Army post names Korag, in Yousmarg. A forest name, Dudhpathri, appears in officials' accounts in some reports and was not used as the Corps' location. Ranks such as top commander or most wanted differ by outlet and are not stated as fact. No report used here establishes that a body or weapons were recovered. None reports further militants or casualties among the forces, so none are stated. The day is Tuesday, 29 September, from the Chinar Corps post. One Indian Express display line says Wednesday. That paper's own item is dated 29 September. No hour is taken from the conflicting "Tuesday" and "Tuesday evening" lines.

## What's next

What the Army account confirms is a joint search, one terrorist neutralised, and an operation then still in progress. The alleged identity, the alleged Lashkar-e-Taiba link, and the setback claim remain officials' words until the Army or another authority states them in its own account.
