Neeru Dhanda won the women's trap individual gold at the 2026 Asian Games in Aichi-Nagoya on 29 September. The reports describe her as the first Indian woman to win Asian Games gold in that event. She is 26 and from Jind.

She also serves in the Army. The same account calls her a Naib Subedar in the Corps of Military Police, and says she trains at the Army Marksmanship Unit in Mhow. Those lines sit with the result. They are part of what PTI copy carried by The Hindu and Sportstar, India Today, and The Indian Express reported together.

Earlier on the same day she won women's trap team silver with Aashima Ahlawat and Manisha Keer. The reports place that team medal before the individual gold. They name the three shooters. The account used here does not add a separate story of how the team final was decided. The confirmed line is the silver, the event, the day, and the two partners beside Dhanda.

The individual gold is also India's fifth gold of these Games, as the reports count it. That is a reported place in the tally, not a table rebuilt for this article. The earlier golds are not named here. What is stated is the number the outlets gave: fifth.

The Hindu and Sportstar carried the PTI copy. India Today and The Indian Express reported the same result. Checked against those accounts, the shared facts are the individual gold, the first-for-an-Indian-woman line, the date, Aichi-Nagoya, the team silver with Ahlawat and Keer, the fifth gold, and Dhanda's age, district, rank, corps and training unit.

The outlets did not use the same wording for the final tally. Because of that, this account leaves out score figures and any claim of an Asian record. There is no number of targets, no final total, and no record margin. A reader who opens the three reports will not find one settled phrase for the tally. Until that wording matches, the result that can be stated cleanly is the gold, not the arithmetic around it.

Leaving the score out does not put the medal in doubt. The outlets reported the individual gold and the team silver. What they did not settle, in one tally phrase, was the score line and whether that performance was an Asian record. Both are omitted rather than taken from a single paper.

The material checked for this story stops at those agreed lines. It does not supply a later start, and this article does not add one. The picture it does fix is the shooter, two medals on 29 September, and the reported place of the individual gold in India's count for these Games.
