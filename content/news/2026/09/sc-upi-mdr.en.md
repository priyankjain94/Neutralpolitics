The Supreme Court on Monday asked the Centre to explain a merchant discount rate on specified UPI payments. India Today and CNBC-TV18 reported that the court declined to pause it. A bench of Chief Justice Surya Kant, Justice Joymalya Bagchi and Justice V. Mohana heard a public-interest petition by advocate Anjan Datta, who argued with advocate Ashutosh Dubey, Hindustan Times reported. CNBC-TV18 spells the name Dutta.

Hindustan Times reported the court seeking the Centre's response. The Chief Justice said: "This is less of a legal and more of a technical issue. We need you (Centre) to state the facts on a short affidavit." The bench asked what executive power supports the charge, and said that if it is a fee it cannot rest on an executive order alone. Once the reply is in, the court said, it can look at in whose hands the money is "income." That report does not say a stay was granted or refused.

India Today and CNBC-TV18 did address the stay. India Today said the petitioner asked for one and the court declined an immediate stay. CNBC-TV18 said the court refused an interim stay, issued notice to the finance ministry, and told the Centre to explain the rationale. India Today said the court also asked the Centre, the Reserve Bank of India and NPCI for the legal basis, for who earns the fee, and for how the money is shared. It said the court has not taken a final view and will look again after affidavits. CNBC-TV18 said implementation has not been stayed. The later of the gathered reports, Hindustan Times, centres on the affidavit. The other two add that a stay was declined. None of the three says the court held the charge lawful.

The fee, as all three describe it, covers specified person-to-merchant UPI payments above ₹2,000, at 0.4 per cent, from 15 October. Hindustan Times linked it to a finance ministry notification of 14 September. India Today and CNBC-TV18 said the plea also challenges a framework announced on 15 September. The cap is ₹300. Hindustan Times said that cap is for transactions above ₹75,000. India Today and CNBC-TV18 said it covers payments of ₹75,000 and above. Person-to-person transfers stay free at any amount. Payments of ₹2,000 or less stay outside the fee. India Today wrote that nearly six years of fully free UPI had ended, and in the same article kept person-to-person transfers and smaller payments outside the charge.

Additional Solicitor General N. Venkataraman said the decision takes effect on 15 October. He said 96 per cent of persons using the UPI gateway are exempt, and that essential services among the remaining 4 per cent are capped. He said not one rupee goes to the government. He called it a settlement fee between the bank and the service provider, which NPCI facilitates. India Today and CNBC-TV18 reported a flat ₹5 on payments above ₹2,000 in railways, telecom, insurance, fuel and agricultural inputs. CNBC-TV18 reported 0.02 per cent, capped at ₹300, for mutual funds, securities, stockbrokers and dealers.

The petition alleges inadequate safeguards, transparency and consultation. It asks the court to quash or suspend the framework above ₹2,000, or to send it back for consultation and an impact study. CNBC-TV18 reported a challenge to amended Section 10A of the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007, and a complaint that RuPay debit cards stay free with no ceiling. The plea's figures, in that report, put person-to-person transfers at 37 per cent of UPI volume and 70 per cent of value.

## What's next

The notification is due to take effect on 15 October. India Today and CNBC-TV18 reported that the court has not stayed that date. The Centre has been asked for an affidavit on the facts and the legal basis. The reports name no next hearing date, and they do not say the court has decided whether the charge is legal.
