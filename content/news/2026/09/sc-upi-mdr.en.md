The Supreme Court on Monday refused an interim stay on the Centre's plan to levy a 0.4 percent merchant discount rate on specified UPI person-to-merchant payments above ₹2,000 from 15 October. A merchant discount rate is a fee charged to the merchant, not an extra charge described here as falling on the customer.

The bench was led by Chief Justice Surya Kant, with Justices Joymalya Bagchi and V. Mohana. The court issued notices to the Centre, the Reserve Bank of India and the National Payments Corporation of India, and sought affidavits in about four weeks on the legal basis of the charge and on who earns it. The bench called the dispute more technical than purely legal, and asked for the executive's legal source, noting that if it is a fee it cannot rest on executive fiat alone. The Additional Solicitor General said not a rupee of it goes to the government, and described it as a settlement fee among the bank and the provider through NPCI.

Payments of ₹75,000 and above are capped at ₹300. Essential and thin-margin sectors pay a flat ₹5. UPI payments in the capital market are set at 0.02 percent, capped at ₹300. Person-to-person transfers stay free at any amount, as do payments of ₹2,000 or less. The government says about 96 percent of users are outside the levy.

The public-interest petition is by advocate Anjan Datta and challenges the framework notified around 14–15 September. It alleges inadequate statutory safeguards and consultation. The court has not ruled on the merits. An earlier listing, before Monday's hearing, was only a listing. It was not itself a ruling, and the fee has not been struck down.
